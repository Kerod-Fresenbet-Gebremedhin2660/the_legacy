Chapter 87: Additional Epilogue - The September Train

Supplementary epilogue written for this edition with AI assistance, at the reader's request.

Nine years later

Lily had refused help with her trunk until they reached the station. Now she was quite willing to let her father push the trolley, provided he understood that she could have managed perfectly well on her own.

Daphne let the matter rest. They had arrived early, both children had their tickets, and nothing had been left behind that couldn't be sent by owl. She was almost certain of the last part. James had packed his own trunk for his second year, which meant that somewhere under his Quidditch things there might even be some schoolbooks.

"We should go through now," he told them, glancing towards the barrier. "The compartments at the back fill up first."

"Then we'll try the front," their father replied.

James gave him a pained look. A year at Hogwarts had apparently been enough to make his father's ignorance embarrassing.

"I said I'd meet the others."

"We have time." Their father steered the trolley around a family who had stopped in the middle of the concourse. "You and Mum first. Lily?"

Lily stepped closer to her father. She had come to see James off last September and had spent most of the journey home complaining that she hadn't been allowed on the train. Today she kept looking at the clock.

James went through the barrier with his mother. The familiar noise met her on the other side: owls protesting, trunks scraping, and voices competing to be heard over the steam escaping from the engine. A boy was trying to haul a cage almost as large as himself along the platform. His mother followed with his luggage, apparently resigned to being less interesting than whatever was inside it.

Lily emerged beside her father, holding the side of the trolley. For a moment she simply stared at the Hogwarts Express. Then she let go and began searching the carriage windows.

"Which one?" she asked James.

"Further down."

He set off, and Lily hurried after him. Harry caught Daphne's eye before pushing the trolley after them.

"She's been waiting all summer," he said.

"Since James got his letter, I think."

It had been difficult to be the one left behind. Lily had endured a year of hearing about places she couldn't visit, people she hadn't met, and jokes that James assured her she would understand when she was older. Eleven, apparently, was old enough. Daphne hoped her son would remember that.

They found a carriage that met James's requirements. He climbed aboard to check the compartments, leaving Lily on the platform with her parents.

"Your wand?" her mother asked.

Lily touched her coat pocket. "Here."

"And your—"

"Mum."

Daphne closed her mouth. Yes, well. They had checked before leaving the house. She had watched Lily check again in the car. Asking a third time wasn't going to improve matters.

Their father lifted James's trunk onto the train. Lily moved to help with hers, but the step was too high for her to get a useful grip. He waited while she tried, then took the other end without comment. Between them, they managed it.

A woman passing with a small girl slowed as she recognized him. Daphne braced herself for the usual interruption, but the woman only nodded and continued along the platform. The girl was more interested in Lily's new owl, which had put its head under a wing and was making a determined effort to ignore everyone.

"Found them!" James called from inside.

Lily picked up the cage. "Is there room for me?"

"Yes."

"You said I couldn't sit with you."

"I said you couldn't sit with me all year. There's a girl in there who's starting too. You can talk to her."

"I can talk to you."

"I know you can."

Daphne hid a smile. He took the cage from Lily, carried it inside, and returned for the trunks. His sister watched through the doorway, trying to see past him.

"You don't have to stay in that compartment," her father told her. "If you meet someone you'd rather sit with, you can."

"I know."

She hesitated, glancing up at the windows again.

"Will people ask about you?"

Her father rested a hand on the trolley. "Some might."

"James said they kept asking him things."

"For a bit," James said, reappearing in the doorway. "Then they found out he wasn't going to come to school with me."

His father looked up at him. "That must have been disappointing."

"Not for me."

Lily laughed, but Daphne noticed that she was still waiting for an answer.

"You can tell them to ask me," Lily's father said. "You don't have to answer for me."

"They'll think I'm being rude."

"Some of them," her mother conceded. "You can still say it."

Lily considered that. She was usually quite capable of being rude to her brother, but strangers were a different matter. Daphne remembered having to learn that distinction herself, though her own mother had been rather more concerned about which strangers she might offend.

A shrill whistle interrupted them. Further along the platform, a father was trying to finish a conversation through a window while his son nodded at everything he said. Daphne glanced at the clock. They still had a few minutes, but James had come down the steps and was looking expectantly at his father.

"Right," his father said, pulling him into a hug.

James endured it with more patience than his mother had expected. He was growing quickly; by next September he might be tall enough to look her in the eye. For now, she could still straighten his collar when he turned to her, much to his annoyance.

"Write," she told him.

"I did last year."

"When you wanted something."

"Not every time."

"Then you'll find it quite easy to do again."

He grinned, hugged her, and pulled away before she could inspect anything else.

Lily had gone quiet. Her mother held out a hand, and Lily stepped into her arms, pressing her face against her shoulder. She held on tightly enough that her mother had to swallow before speaking.

"You can write even if you don't want anything," she said quietly.

Lily nodded against her. A moment later she drew back, rubbing her nose with the back of her hand. Daphne decided to overlook it.

Lily's father bent to hug her next. Lily said something her mother couldn't hear, and he nodded before letting her go.

"James?" Lily asked.

He had reached the doorway again. "What?"

"You'll show me where to go when we get there?"

"First-years go a different way. I'll show you who to follow."

"Oh."

He paused, then came down a step and held out his hand for her bag.

"Come on. I'll introduce you."

That helped. Lily handed it over and turned to wave once more before following him aboard.

Daphne stayed beside Harry while the remaining doors were shut. Through the window she could see James moving a pile of magazines off a seat. Lily settled beside another girl, who leaned forward to look at the owl. Already there was something to discuss.

It was easier to watch that than to look at the empty trolley.

There had been mornings when Daphne had wanted nothing more than five quiet minutes without an argument outside her bedroom. Over breakfast Lily had accused James of hiding her ticket, only to discover it in the pocket where she had put it. In a few hours Daphne would probably miss even that. Well, perhaps not the accusation itself. The noise, certainly.

Harry stood close enough for their shoulders to touch. He was watching the same window.

"What did she ask you?" Daphne said.

"Whether we'd both come at Christmas. To meet the train."

"Of course we will."

"That's what I told her."

The engine began to pull away. Lily saw the platform moving and scrambled to the window, waving with both hands. James appeared behind her, lifted one hand, and then seemed to think better of looking so casual. He waved properly as they passed.

Daphne kept her hand raised until she could no longer pick out their window. Around them parents began collecting younger children and abandoned trolleys. Someone was crying; someone else was complaining about having to get back to work. An owl hooted indignantly from a cage that had somehow missed the train.

She thought of Tracey, briefly and with the old ache. Her friend would have enjoyed knowing every detail of this morning, particularly the ones Daphne would have preferred to leave out. There were still things she caught herself wanting to tell her.

Beside her, Harry reached for the trolley. Neither of them had moved very far.

"Breakfast?" he suggested.

"You've had breakfast."

"You didn't."

She had been too busy making sure everyone else had eaten. Or that had been her excuse, anyway. She looked back along the tracks once more, then took his free hand.

"Somewhere nearby," she said. "I don't feel like going home just yet."

"All right."

They joined the slow queue towards the barrier. Ahead of them the owner of the forgotten owl was explaining herself to a station attendant. Harry glanced over, and Daphne tightened her grip on his hand.

"She's already being helped."

"I can see that."

He was smiling when he turned back to her. Daphne allowed herself a small smile in return and led him towards breakfast.
