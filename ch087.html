Chapter 87: An Evening Out

Supplementary chapter written for this edition with AI assistance, at the reader's request.

One year later

Daphne had almost forgotten what it was like to leave the house without having to find somebody's shoes first. Her own were exactly where she had left them. Harry was ready, and neither of the children was there to object to anything. It was suspiciously easy.

"We haven't forgotten something, have we?" she asked.

"Probably," Harry replied, offering her coat. "But your mother has enough things to keep them occupied until tomorrow."

"That's what worries me. James had already found the sweet tin when we left."

"Your father was helping him."

"Yes, I noticed."

Harry held her coat while she slipped her arms into it, then moved her hair clear of the collar. His fingers lingered at the back of her neck. Daphne leaned into the touch for a moment before turning to face him.

"Ready?"

"Nearly." He kissed her, briefly at first, then again when she caught his sleeve. "Now I am."

They arrived at the restaurant a little early. It was a small place off Diagon Alley, with a narrow dining room and enough space between the tables that Daphne wouldn't have to learn everything about the people beside them. She had asked for a table away from the entrance. The witch who took their coats remembered, and led them to an alcove towards the back.

Harry waited for Daphne to choose a seat before sitting beside her. She put her gloves aside and opened the menu.

"This looks promising," he said.

"The food or the evening?"

"Both."

She smiled down at the menu. Outside, someone stopped to peer through the window, thought better of it, and moved on. Daphne decided she rather liked the table.

Ron and Hermione arrived together, bringing a short draught of cold air and a disagreement that had evidently begun some distance away.

"It was on the way," Hermione was saying as they approached.

"It was two streets in the other direction."

"From the other Floo connection."

"Which we didn't use."

Hermione greeted them while Ron took her coat. She turned back to him to free a strand of hair caught under the collar, then caught his hand and squeezed it before sitting down.

"We're not late, are we?"

"No," Daphne assured her. "Bookshop?"

Ron nodded, hanging the coat on the stand behind their seats. "She said ten minutes."

"I was only looking for one thing."

"That doesn't make it quicker," Daphne pointed out. "In my experience, it makes it much worse."

"Thank you," Ron said, sitting down with an expression of relief. "I've been trying to explain that."

Hermione opened her mouth to object, but Harry got there first.

"She's right. I've waited outside enough shops to know."

Daphne glanced at him. "You've also waited inside, talked to the owner, and made us considerably later."

"I was being polite."

"He wanted to know how the display shelves moved," she told Hermione.

"Well, that's different," Hermione said at once, earning a groan from Ron.

The waitress came to take their drinks order, interrupting what might otherwise have become a lengthy discussion. When she left, Ron leaned towards Hermione.

"I didn't mind stopping," he said more quietly. "You could just have said you wanted to."

"I did want to."

"I know. You were still looking at the window when we crossed the road."

She looked at him for a moment, then smiled and moved her chair closer to his. "Thank you."

Ron looked pleased with himself. Sensibly, he didn't spoil it by asking whether this meant he had won the argument.

Once they had ordered, the conversation turned to Daphne's week. A department had returned three boxes to the archives and demanded a document which, according to its own receipt, it still possessed. The correspondence was becoming unpleasantly familiar.

"Have you sent them a copy of the receipt?" Hermione asked.

"Twice."

"And they still deny having it?"

"They say it's proof the document was taken out, not proof that it hasn't been returned."

Ron snorted into his drink. "Send them the boxes back."

"I'd thought of that. Unfortunately, I want the boxes less than I want an answer."

"Don't send the boxes, then. Send whoever keeps writing the letters down to look through them."

Daphne paused with her glass halfway to her mouth. "That might actually work."

"It's what I'd do."

She smiled at him. "I'll let you know."

Hermione rested her elbow on the table. "Or you could arrange a proper review of their borrowing records."

"I could," Daphne agreed. "I'd probably still be conducting it at Christmas."

"And she'd miss dinner," Harry added.

Daphne glanced at him. He had been listening, then. For the last few minutes he had looked distracted, his hand straying towards his pocket whenever the conversation paused.

The food arrived before she could ask. For a while they were occupied with making room for plates and passing things across the table. Harry remembered the sauce she had wanted to try and moved it within reach before she asked. She thanked him with a touch to his arm.

Ron and Hermione had chosen different dishes after a prolonged discussion, and almost immediately began exchanging forkfuls. Hermione was explaining why his choice had sounded too rich when she stopped, considered the taste, and took another.

"Terrible, isn't it?" Ron asked.

"I didn't say terrible."

He pushed his plate a little closer. "Here."

Hermione shook her head, smiling, and offered him some of hers instead.

Daphne turned to Harry to draw his attention to them, only to find him unfolding a small piece of parchment beside his plate.

"Harry."

"Hm? Sorry. I'm listening."

"I hadn't said anything yet."

He looked up properly. His hand stopped moving, leaving the parchment half-open.

"Is that needed tonight?" she asked.

"No. It's for Monday. I just thought of something that might—"

"Then it can wait."

His mouth tightened. "I only need a moment."

Daphne put down her fork. They had found an evening when all four of them could come, arranged for the children to stay with her parents, and booked the table weeks ago. She didn't want to spend it waiting for him to look up.

"You said that before we left work yesterday," she told him quietly. "I waited nearly half an hour. I'd like to have dinner with you."

He glanced at the parchment. Across the table, Hermione had become very interested in the pepper mill. Ron took it from her and passed it to Harry without comment.

For a moment Daphne thought Harry would try to finish the note anyway. Instead, he folded it and put it away.

"You're right. Sorry."

"Thank you."

She picked up her fork again. She was still a little annoyed, but didn't see what another argument would improve. Harry reached for his glass, hesitated, and turned back to her.

"I was wondering whether I'd missed something in the report. I keep going over it."

"Will you be any better at finding it while eating?"

"Probably not."

"Then eat. You can read it again tomorrow."

He nodded and moved his chair a little closer to hers. When she rested her hand on his knee, he covered it briefly with his own.

Ron broke the pause by asking whether they had settled on somewhere for their summer holiday. Harry's expression changed at once; evidently, this was a subject he had been waiting for someone to raise.

"We've narrowed it down," Daphne said before he could speak.

"To anywhere without a Ministry office," Harry added.

"That still leaves quite a lot of choice," Hermione pointed out.

"I want somewhere we can walk," Daphne said. "Somewhere the children can get tired without breaking anything expensive."

"Beach," Ron suggested.

"Sand in everything," Hermione replied.

"Worth it."

"You said that last time. I was still finding it in my bag a fortnight later."

Ron grinned. "You liked it."

"I liked most of it."

The account that followed involved a missed turning, a very long walk, and a picnic that had been much better than either of them had expected. Ron remembered the food and an excellent view; Hermione remembered which path they should have taken. Neither seemed particularly sorry they had missed it.

Daphne listened, enjoying the warmth of Harry's leg against hers. His attention had returned to the table. Once, when Ron appealed to him for support, he declined with the excuse that he hadn't been present and couldn't possibly judge. Hermione looked so surprised that Daphne laughed before she could stop herself.

"You might try that more often," she told him.

"I'm trying it now."

By the time their plates were cleared, Daphne had stopped wondering whether he would reach for the parchment again.

Harry ordered a pear tart. Daphne chose the chocolate pudding, considered the description a second time, and asked for another spoon.

"For me?" Harry asked.

"If you're willing to share yours."

"I hadn't agreed to that."

"You can negotiate."

Ron laughed. "Careful. I remember where that got you last time."

Hermione gave him a warning glance, but Daphne found herself smiling.

"We were very thorough," she said.

"You were," Harry agreed. "I spent most of the time asking what everyone was talking about."

"And then asking why you should agree to it."

"Reasonable question."

She touched his hand beneath the table. "It was."

He looked at her, his smile quietening a little. Daphne held his gaze. Their beginning still wasn't something she could turn into an amusing story from first word to last, but she could remember the two of them sitting opposite each other, convinced they knew exactly how little they wanted from the arrangement.

Hermione reached for her glass. "I remember getting considerably less time to prepare."

"You made up for it with questions," Ron told her.

"Someone had to ask them."

"I know." He leaned over and kissed her cheek. "You still do."

Hermione tried to look displeased with him, without much success. When she turned to return the kiss, Ron caught her hand against his chest for a moment before letting her settle back into her seat.

Daphne was glad to leave it there when the puddings arrived. Harry turned his plate towards her, offering the better half of the tart, and she pushed her pudding between them.

They stayed until the other tables began to empty. Hermione asked for the name of the wine, and Ron wrote it on the back of the receipt for her. Harry leaned back in his chair with his arm resting behind Daphne. When she shifted closer, his hand settled at her shoulder.

Outside, Ron handed over the receipt. Hermione tucked it into her bag, then turned back to Daphne.

"We should do this again," she said.

"We should," Daphne agreed. "Before another month disappears."

They settled on a date, with Hermione making a note of it and Ron checking that he hadn't promised the same evening to someone else. There was a round of goodbyes, an embrace from Hermione that no longer surprised Daphne, and then the two of them set off towards the public Floo.

Harry offered Daphne his arm. "Home?"

She slipped her hand through it. "Not quite yet. Let's walk a little."

They turned away from the busier end of the street. Most of the shops were shut, their windows dark apart from the occasional lamp left burning over a display. Daphne slowed beside a window full of children's robes, saw Harry watching her, and shook her head.

"Tomorrow. Tonight I'm not buying anything."

"I wasn't going to say anything."

"You were smiling."

"I like seeing you enjoy yourself."

She looked away from the window and found she was smiling too. "I did."

"Even with the interruption?"

"I was cross with you for about five minutes, Harry. It doesn't mean I stopped enjoying your company."

He nodded, looking down the street. She let him think for a few steps before moving her hand from his arm to his.

"You can tell me if something's worrying you," she added. "I'd rather hear it than sit there guessing."

"I know."

"Yes, well. Occasionally you forget."

His fingers tightened around hers. "I didn't want to bring work to dinner."

"You managed rather badly, then."

He laughed, and she leaned against him as they walked. Once she might have spent the whole meal saying nothing, waiting for him to notice, and been furious when he didn't. She didn't miss it. Nor, she suspected, did he.

At the next corner, Harry stopped and turned towards her.

"I had something else I wanted to say before we left."

"Should I be worried?"

"I don't think so. I like going out with you. Just the two of us, or like tonight. I know we've been busy, but I'd like to do it more often."

Daphne looked at him. They had made a habit of finding time for the children, for work, for friends who needed something. Somewhere along the way, being in the same room at the end of the day had begun to count as an evening together.

"So would I," she said.

"I'll book somewhere."

"I'd like that."

She put her free hand against his cheek and kissed him. He stepped closer, his hand warm at her waist, and for a while neither of them seemed particularly interested in finding the Floo.

When she drew back, Harry was smiling at her in a way that still made it difficult to remember what she had intended to say.

"Next week?" he asked.

She nodded. "And you can leave the report at home."

"I was hoping to get it finished before then."

Daphne laughed and kissed him once more before letting him take her arm again. They walked on slowly, with no need to hurry back just yet.
