Chapter 20: A Possibility

On Saturday evening, after a sleepless night and a day spent double-checking his reading, Harry approached the Headmaster's office with his notes. He had cancelled practice and skipped homework with his friends. How hadn't he thought of it before? Not simple, nor obvious, but all the clues had been there. He doubted anyone had tried it. His situation had to be unique.

He hadn't reached the top of the stairs when the door opened and the Headmaster smiled down at him.

"Harry," he greeted, "I hadn't expected you but I'm very pleased to see you again. Do come in!"

"Yes, sir," Harry replied with a slight nod.

"You have gathered the memory, then?" Dumbledore asked with twinkling eyes.

Harry blinked, too surprised to understand the question. Then, he understood. "Oh, no, sir."

"Harry," the Headmaster frowned, looking for the first time to the parchment his student had brought with him, "I made it abundantly clear just how important that memory is, haven't I?"

"Yes, sir, you have, but that's not why I came here," Harry waved off and dropped his notes on the desk. Turning to his Headmaster, he said, "This is about me being a Horcrux."

Dumbledore's eyes widened, and he paled dramatically. Harry meanwhile looked through the parchment he had brought, rearranging them once more.

"I'm sorry," Dumbledore said heavily. "I never intended for you to find out like that. I tried to find a solution without burdening you with the truth. Please believe I had your well-being in mind."

"I guessed I'd die in this war last June. With the fragment in me, I merely have to. Knowing allowed me to prepare. And I haven't lost that much trust. We're just working together – you made that clear. I can't lose what I don't have."

Dumbledore paled further and swayed. Harry knew that had hurt. But the admission of the Headmaster's secrecy stung too much to heed the small voice in the back of his mind.

"Nice of you to admit it, that speeds this up." Harry moved the topmost parchment to the back. "We know Horcruxes need to be destroyed. I don't like the prospect, and I think I've found another solution for the fragment in me."

Dumbledore stared. Harry smiled. "No, I enjoy this too much to tell you outright. We'll do it your way. Horcruxes are destroyed by highly destructive magic, substances, creatures... or regret. For a living Horcrux, I assume death counts too?" Dumbledore nodded.

"Regret is different, and only the creator can use it. You told me in November that the fragments try to become whole again. I think the magic keeps them apart, a barrier between pieces meant to be one. If the creator regrets his deeds, that barrier breaks and the pull tears the fragment from its container."

"A bold theory, Harry," Dumbledore pointed out with a raised eyebrow.

"Do you think I'm mistaken?"

"... no," the Headmaster said. "I have guessed so myself. But Voldemort will not regret his actions."

"I don't expect him to. But when his soul reunites, he can't be possessed by his past self; they mix. Sunshine, unicorns prancing, whatever. Possession might be an unsuccessful reunion: the fragment grows stronger than the victim's soul and overwhelms it."

"Why hasn't mine possessed me? The diary needed only weeks; I've carried a fragment for years. I even share its Parseltongue. Something keeps us apart. A barrier."

"Your mother's sacrifice, yes," Dumbledore agreed. "But it doesn't help us remove the fragment from you, unfortunately."

"Our goal is to destroy the Horcrux, not necessarily remove the fragment. I don't want to destroy the container – me. Parts of a soul in the same body, kept apart by a force... it sounds like a shattered mind."

Dumbledore jumped to his feet. "No! Harry, we cannot do what you are thinking of!" the Headmaster yelled. "To toy with the fragile balance or with your mind like that on a mere whim would be madness!"

His student smiled. "It's not a whim. Separate minds in one body can be reunited by a Summersby-Tsareva Bridge. A skilled Legilimens opens a path and lets them flow together. Mind and soul are linked. Why couldn't it work with a soul?"

"A modified bridge could work," Dumbledore sighed. "But these aren't two parts meant to be one. It's a parasitic fragment kept at bay. You are proposing a deliberate attempt to have yourself possessed."

Harry pointed to his notes. "If we work quickly enough, my soul should overwhelm the fragment before it can possess me. It would be diffused within me and cease to exist as part of Voldemort. I'd be possessing it, instead of the other way around."

"But you would cease to exist as well. You wouldn't be the same Harry Potter. You're proposing assisted suicide with a real chance of creating a new Tom Riddle in your body."

The instruments huffed and puffed as they looked at each other. Harry shook his head. "I know I'd change. But if the alternative is dying, I'm willing to risk it. The faster the integration, the more of myself should remain. We might get a slightly altered Harry Potter, but ultimately still me. People change, sir."

"They grow up, Harry, which is not the same," the Headmaster replied.

"Why should I be like everyone else?" Harry joked without humour. "I want Voldemort out of me. If we can deal with this now, while there's time to prepare, I want to try. Do you think it could work?"

Dumbledore studied him, then held out his healthy hand. Reluctantly, Harry passed over the notes. He should have made a copy.

The Headmaster read, comparing annotations and theories. Eventually, Fawkes came over to Harry, who petted him and whispered compliments. Perhaps it was a bird trait; Hedwig liked compliments too.

"Remarkable work," Dumbledore said at last. "But it wouldn't work. You lack important knowledge, mainly Arithmancy."

"It always leads back to that, doesn't it?" Harry asked wryly.

"It does, yes. Your plan is decades ahead of what you could have read. But you've miscalculated."

"Are you sure?" Harry questioned with a raised eyebrow of his own. "You're not just trying to discourage me from my course of action because you dislike the method?"

Dumbledore frowned deeply. "I'm insulted you think so lowly of me. I don't approve, but I'm still an educator. Your theory would cost you your life without a chance of success."

Harry pursed his lips. "And could you help me work out the flaws?"

"You are rather set on this, aren't you?" the Headmaster asked.

"Most students worry about marks. I worry about surviving to sit the tests," Harry said. "If we solve this ahead of time, I could focus on a war I might even survive."

"Helping you correct it would take years," Dumbledore said. "I wouldn't live to see it."

"Oh," Harry sighed. "Well, that's... well..."

"I could," the Headmaster continued in a hollow, tired voice, "solve them myself."

Harry blinked in surprise. "You... you could? But you just said... you could?"

"I have almost a century of knowledge, Harry. Teaching you would take too long. Doing it myself, I could avoid the delays and explanations. But I dread the day you ask me to assist, and I cannot let anyone else do it. No one must discover Voldemort's secret."

They looked at each other for another moment. Fawkes stared between the two, studying them. Finally, he decided and turned back to Harry, nudging him.

Dumbledore smiled sadly. "Well, that settles it, then. If Fawkes thinks so, then I will do it. Let us write history, then, with this foolish endeavour."

"The Dumbledore-Potter Bridge," Harry replied, equally saddened. He had come to force matters. Seeing the Headmaster's reluctance dampened his happiness.

"The Potter-Dumbledore Bridge, Harry. It has a nicer ring to it, not that it will ever see publication."

On Tuesday evening, Harry ate a small dinner before another short practice. He needed distraction. Dumbledore was correcting the research; the procedure was drawing nearer. Theorizing about a chance to live had been one thing. Knowing the experiment would actually happen was another.

He had half an hour to reach the pitch. As Captain he should be early, but it was freezing outside. Ron and Peakes might be late anyway, and Katie had threatened him if he endangered her N.E.W.T.s. He chose the longest route without making a detour. He wasn't wasting time, merely on his way. That was his story.

The deserted corridor had once held classes no longer funded. Hermione had told them Muggle Studies combined the old Muggle Culture and Muggle Technology classes. Neither subject fitted the reduced time, leaving a muddle of half-truths. No wonder wizards knew so little about Muggles.

There had been Wizarding Culture too, to teach purebloods about their own government, traditions and history.

Passing Paul and Agnes, the arguing but silenced stone figures, Harry noticed Slytherin green in a dark alcove. His eyes adjusted. Greengrass. It wasn't dawdling if he did something worthwhile. He walked over; she tensed.

"Hello, Greengrass," he greeted, keeping his voice low.

"Leave. I don't have any use for you." She turned even further away.

Harry rolled his eyes. "Nice view, don't you think? So... dark and gloomy."

She turned to him, her eyes shining with anger. "I told you to leave, Potter. Don't make me repeat myself."

"I saw you and wanted to know how you're doing. Just being friendly." But he grew worried. The rings under her eyes and ashen complexion didn't suit her.

He leant forward, closer to her. "Everything alright with you?"

"Not your business," she snapped, glaring at him.

"You don't look all that well, and as the resident hero, it is kind of my job to worry about people, you know?" he tried.

"Rich, coming from you," she replied with a meaningful glance. It was true. The procedure weighed on him, and the nightmares had returned.

"And," she added after a moment, "you aren't much of a hero. Puny, short-tempered, biased against a fourth of the school..."

"Yeah, but no one can stand those Ravenclaws," Harry told her with a shrug.

"You aren't even funny. Nothing like the books," she said. "And you certainly don't look the part."

"I'll keep it in mind. A bit of sleep and I'll be fine."

"I doubt that. No amount of sleep will make you fine. And I don't care what you do; I already told you to leave so I didn't ask you about your well-being," she replied, bored with him.

"Well, if there is something I can do, you know where to find me, alright?" He made to leave, but hesitated.

"What now?" she asked, annoyed.

He sighed. Curse his good heart, he couldn't let her run into danger. "Be careful around Malfoy, he might be dangerous..."

Her stare made him fall silent. It was as if she stared right into his soul, as if she tried to uncover all his secrets. Finally, she nodded. "Don't worry. He's keeping to himself. And I can look out for myself as well."

"I'm not just worried about him," Harry began.

"But about his master as well, yes," she finished for him with another nod.

Harry almost wanted to hug her. Not for anything sensible, but because he wasn't alone. She had seen it too.

"Yes," he whispered. Then, louder, "Yes! I'm not crazy! He is? I knew it!"

Daphne peered at him warily. He might have seemed crazy, whatever he claimed to the contrary.

"So he's really a Death Eater then?" Harry asked her. "I knew it!"

"I have no evidence either way," she said. "And I don't care. If he is a Death Eater, I'm better off not opposing him. The less I know, the better. It's not my war."

"So he just keeps to himself?" he asked her.

She sounded irritated when she answered, "He talks to people, just not me. And I don't care about that. I'm quite happy to not have him in my life, remember?"

"Well... I hope you'll get better." He left her in the darkened alcove.

"... and you can't write it like that, Ron. You make it sound as if it is an unavoidable conclusion when in reality it is only a reasonable assumption." Their Wednesday quarrel had arrived, it seemed, and Harry had to bite his tongue to not jump in.

"Yeah, well, it sounded like it was," the redhead grumbled. "This stuff doesn't make any sense."

"It does make sense," she insisted, pinching the bridge of her nose. "They're plants, living beings. Cutting them back that far would take years to correct, but some surprise you..."

"Malfoy's a Death Eater," Harry interrupted. They were secluded, with privacy charms in place. He couldn't stand their arguing when there were more important things to discuss.

"Not this again, Harry!" Hermione groaned.

"We are kind of busy right now," Ron told him with a roll of his eyes.

"Daphne thinks so too. I ran into her yesterday. She's not doing so well, but..."

"Does she know?" Hermione narrowed her eyes. "Did she say she was sure, or are you assuming?"

"She said she had no proof either way," he told her truthfully.

"Well, there you go," Hermione pointed out, "she doesn't know either. And, Harry, he's not one, you are just seeing things."

"I disagree. It is actually the logical conclusion," Harry said.

"No, it's not," Hermione disagreed. "Don't you see? You just want him to be a Death Eater so you have something against him. He's sixteen, Harry. He's a student."

"He's nearly an adult. His father, aunt and uncle are Death Eaters, and he grew up with their ideas. Would it surprise you if he supported them?"

"Connections aren't a motive," she said. "Nor are parents the only influence. Why would he take that risk?"

"You want a motive for Malfoy to join?" Harry asked. "He's a bully."

"How would being a bully lead to him joining the Death Eaters?" Hermione demanded.

"Remember Buckbeak? He ignored the instructions, then enjoyed having his father push for an execution. Or when he insulted Cedric on the train, just to hurt us?"

"He went a bit overboard, but could easily have learned his lesson. This year, he hasn't acted up at all," Hermione told him resolutely.

"Last year he abused his Prefect and Inquisitorial Squad powers too. I grew up at the Dursleys, Hermione. I know bullies. Take something from them, and some learn their lesson. Others look for revenge and someone else to back them. He lost his father, his money, his power. Voldemort offers all that back, and asks for loyalty and a few dead Muggles. A bargain, to someone like Malfoy."

"Still speculation. No evidence," she said. "And what would Voldemort get? What does Malfoy offer, stuck at school, that is... of... ?" She slowed, paling, and locked eyes with Harry. He frowned, then caught on.

Ron stared between the two of them. "What's up with you? I don't get it."

Hermione shook her head, slowly at first, then more frantically. "No. He... he wouldn't. They wouldn't! They... No! That's... no..." Her hands found their way to her hair, and she gripped it, pulling fiercely.

"Would either of you explain to me what you are talking about?" Ron complained.

"He needs to recruit," Hermione told Ron with a shudder. "He has giants, Dementors and Death Eaters, but needs more wands. Malfoy has students around him nine months a year. He can find candidates, poison their minds out of reach of the staff..."

She stopped, blinked, then cursed so loudly and violently that Harry and Ron gaped.

"And here I thought you didn't listen to me," Ron told her once the shock had worn off. She glared at him before turning to pace. He shrugged and mouthed to Harry, "Worth it."

"He wouldn't do that. He wouldn't, right, Harry? Please tell me he wouldn't!" She looked positively deranged. "He wouldn't use... He can't... He..."

Ron gently led her to a seat. Harry thought it over. "I fear he would, Hermione. Sorry."

"But..." She gripped her hair. "Learning! He can't have..."

"He could use tutoring to recruit outside his house. Heads over books, away from the teachers. A perfect chance to sniff them out."

"But that's tutoring! Learning! He can't use it for his own purposes! ARGH!" She jumped up and let loose another bout of curses.

"So, he's doing something, then?" Ron asked.

"We don't know," Hermione sighed. "But it's an obvious plan. The future of the wizarding world in one place. Every student swayed is one less fighting him."

"And a rich pureblood's son would be a good choice," Harry added. "He has a motive, opportunity and knows how to twist words."

"But Voldemort has Snape here," Ron pointed out.

"Snape's a spy," Harry replied. "He has to stay undercover. Besides, if you want to convince someone to become a criminal, don't send authority, send their equals."

They stared at each other. "So, what do we do?" Ron asked. "How do we stop him?"

Hermione bit her lip. "Well, we should tell a teacher, of course. This isn't some school-yard brawl. Don't you remember what Dumbledore said at the start of the school year? If we notice anything at all, we are supposed to tell the staff or the Aurors."

"I'm not sure having the Mark alone is punishable," Harry said. "Snape has one, and he's free. They can't remove it, can they?"

"If the Aurors get him," Ron said, "can't they give him Veritaserum and find out what he did?"

"You can't just give someone Veritaserum," Hermione said. "We have no evidence, not even a Dark Mark. He might not have one at all. Nothing to link him to Voldemort if he's searched."

"He may or may not have joined, with or without a Mark or mission," she summed up. "Not a lot to go on, but too many reasons to dismiss. We should still tell the staff and Aurors."

"Still... someone must have noticed. In first year only we went down there, but the whole school knew exactly what happened. If he's recruiting, surely someone heard something?"

"Let's tell Flitwick or Sprout," Hermione said. "They're Heads of Houses."

Ron nodded slowly. "Flitwick might work. Sprout is... well, a Hufflepuff. Thinking bad of others is... anyway, Flitwick is smart, he'll listen to us."

Harry nodded, staring into the distance. Something she had said bothered him.

"Harry? Something the matter?" Hermione asked.

He jumped up. "Excuse me, I need a quick word with Katie."

He crossed the common room to her table. Ron and Hermione watched one of Katie's friends jump up and leave, crying; another followed. Katie gave Harry a reproachful look before answering. He nodded and returned, then changed course and left through the portrait hole. They stared at each other. Moments later, he came back and they refreshed the privacy charms.

"Well?" she asked him with a raised eyebrow.

Harry looked miserable. "I think I know how it was done. Someone should have noticed, Hermione. Rumours turn the wheels here. Why didn't anyone tell? I talked to the Fat Lady and asked how well paintings see."

"Harry! That's what this was about?" Hermione yelled, too surprised by his admission.

"They don't see the truth like the Map. They see what's visible. To her I'm a black-haired boy in Gryffindor colours, not necessarily Harry Potter. Their sight and hearing seem worse than ours. She can't see under the cloak. They have no sense of smell, and I don't think they can give us Pensieve memories. Remember McLaggen?"

"You asked Katie and her friends about McLaggen?" Hermione interrupted. "They knew him well! How could you be so insensitive?"

"I asked Katie and her year-mates about him. It was important. He told the only painting nearby that he urgently needed help, and it ran off. Shortly after, he fell."

"No one saw him actually jump." Hermione put a hand to her mouth and shook her head.

"Someone might have made him send the painting away. Katie said McLaggen had real trouble shaking off the Imperius when Crouch taught them."

"You... you think someone made him jump?" Hermione asked, her voice far too high for her.

"Not jump, no. Surviving is a stronger impulse than resisting Crouch's embarrassing tricks. He might fight it off at the last moment. But sending a painting away? Someone Disillusioned could stand nearby without the painting noticing. Could you do a Disillusionment charm, Hermione?"

"I guess so. It might not be perfect, but..."

"So Malfoy could be there..."

"You think he silenced a witness," Hermione said with a shaky voice.

"McLaggen might have heard something. Maybe Malfoy tried to recruit him and failed. With the portrait gone, he could stun McLaggen and levitate him over the railing." Harry raised a quill and pointed it toward the table. "Then let him fall. I don't know if a Stunner leaves traces, but the Imperius doesn't. Just blank eyes..."

They sat quietly. Ron struggled with the idea of a classmate killing someone. Hermione shook her head. Harry had already accepted his theory.

"But," Hermione tried, "but Malfoy would have had to have learned it somewhere. For that plan to work, someone must have taught him the Imperius."

"He has Aunt Bellatrix to look out for him, doesn't he?" Ron grumbled.

"But the Decree for the Restriction of Reasonable Use of Underage Sorcery..."

"It checks where magic happens, not who did it," Harry said. "Dobby got me blamed because magic happened at my relatives' house. In Malfoy's house, the adults are meant to make sure he obeys. Can you imagine his aunt doing that?"

"So you think he killed McLaggen to hide evidence?" Hermione asked.

"Yes. And I don't think it was the first. Brooks must have heard or seen something too."

"He could have erased her memories," Hermione pointed out.

"He might not have trusted the spells. Brooks was only in second year when Crouch taught here. He might never have used the Imperius on her. She'd be unprepared. A quick jab, some Firewhiskey smuggled in..." He worked through the idea aloud. "There are no paintings by the Prefects' bathroom. No witnesses. There was Firewhiskey on her clothes and the bottle in the pool. Maybe she spilled some, fought the curse. But he had her drink it. Then undress..."

Hermione shivered violently on the chair, and Ron grabbed her arm to steady her.

Harry pictured it, trying to fill the blanks. "Into the bath, drop the bottle, then drown herself. No pressure marks from being held under. It fits, even if I don't want to believe it."

Hermione whimpered, but it might have been from Ron's grip. It looked painful, at least.

"And he just... left?" Ron asked, his voice nowhere near as strong as usual.

"Don't..." Hermione whispered, too low for Harry to hear.

"No, he couldn't just leave. If she shook off the curse, or someone found her too soon, she might talk. He had to make sure." Ron and Hermione exchanged wary looks. Harry didn't notice; he stared at the wall.

"He couldn't have risked it," he repeated, more quietly, dread stealing into his heart.
