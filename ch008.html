Chapter 8: Boys and Girls

He shouted, jumping abruptly back, refusing what his eyes were telling him. Breaking the surface, he whirled around, towards the stairs, away from... away; he wanted – needed – to get as far away as possible. Back to the house, the dormitory – sleep, and everything would be fine, wouldn't it?

But just a second later and before he had taken the second step, his mind cleared just like it always did in times of danger and his nature set in. His instincts took over, likely dooming him; he turned back around and dove underwater.

It was no mermaid, though he realized why he had thought so at first. The hair floating in the water gave a wild impression, something he had seen in the lake back during the second task. With his heart hammering in his chest, Harry had reached the body within moments. Its eyes were wide open, the mouth slightly ajar. Harry didn't waste any time. He pushed an arm under the body right below the pit, silently thanking his experience with carrying dead weight in water from the Tri-Wizard Tournament. Refusing to notice the lack of response, he pushed towards the surface. The sound of the waves he made echoed ominously off the cold walls. The mermaid had woken from her slumber and watched him, giggling.

Painfully slow, he walked over to the edge of the tub, but with each step, he regained a bit of his feelings. He had to do something, obviously. But he didn't know what to do. He could fight Death Eaters, no doubt about that, but what could he do if he found someone drowning?

He needed help. Why hadn't he asked Hermione to join him, Harry thought desperately, she would probably know what to do. He needed help, and fast.

"Dobby!" he yelled as loud as he could. "Kreacher!"

Only a blink of an eye later, both elves appeared with loud cracks.

Kreacher's "Filthy Half-Breed master called poor... ?" mingled with Dobby's outcry of "The great Harry Po...", before both fell silent in shock.

But Harry didn't wait for them to regain their senses. "Kreacher, get the Headmaster! Dobby, get... Pomfrey!" he shouted the first name that came to him.

The hyperactive elf blinked and disappeared with another loud crack. Kreacher on the other hand sneered for a moment, before he too did as he was told, leaving Harry with a lifeless body and the echoes of his solitary breathing.

Finally, he had reached the edge of the tub, and just as he had, Dobby had returned. "Dobby did as he was told, Master Harry Potter sir. Mistress Pomfrey be coming."

"Then help me, get over here and..." But Dobby had already snapped his fingers, and the body, heavy just a moment ago and threatening to slip from his grip, suddenly jumped out of the water and hung limply in the air a foot above the floor. Without the support of Harry, the head rolled forward and the arms swayed from the movement.

In trance, Harry climbed out of the tub. Numbness had stolen over his body, dampening the sounds. Help was on the way; he had done his part, but he felt dreadfully weak.

The door flew open as the matron burst in. Finally feeling relieved of his responsibility, Harry leaned against the wall and sank to the floor. He had done it, all he could think of. He heard Madam Pomfrey yelling "Elf, get the Heads of Houses!" and Dobby cancelling the levitation spell – the body crumbled to the ground – and popping away.

As Harry's mind cleared further, he noticed the nurse bustling around. She had started to fling spells at the body far faster than Harry had thought her able to. But he couldn't look at her shocked expression and didn't feel strong enough to stand up. His eyes were drawn towards the body on the floor.

She was small, he thought, before realizing with a jolt that they were about the same size. In fact, he had probably walked past her in the corridors in the past, but who was she? He couldn't match the face to a name, maybe refusing to recall one for the lifeless form. And then he realized it was the first time in his life that he was in the presence of a naked girl.

The door flew open once more, and Professor McGonagall stormed inside, followed by a pair of Aurors. She was even paler than Pomfrey, white as marble, but her eyes quickly travelled through the room. With a quick glance in his direction, she fired off a quick spell and Harry found a bathrobe falling on him. He suddenly remembered that he too was naked. The Aurors meanwhile had begun their search of the surroundings just as Professor Sprout and Flitwick arrived.

Even though the air was warm, Harry had started to shiver. Absentmindedly, he stood up and put on the robe he had been given, his eyes still transfixed on the scene in front of him. He had never thought he would see a horrified Flitwick. It didn't fit him. Professor Sprout was rooted to her spot staring disbelievingly at the body. Someone said something, but Harry heard little more than buzzing. In fact, his eyes were once again drawn to the girl lying on the floor. Someone had conjured a towel that now covered the indecent body parts. The girl looked so small. Now that her indecency had been covered, he looked, truly looked at her. He had seen her somewhere before, but she wasn't a Gryffindor or member of the DA. Harry felt relief wash over him, 'not a friend' popped into his head. Only a second later, he hated himself; disgust spreading, he fell to his knees as, nauseated, he heaved. Not a friend, but a student had died. Did she have family? Very likely, he realized with another jolt. Her friends had to be informed, and where in the world was Dumbledore? Slowly, he got up, swaying slightly.

He watched the body, waiting for the girl to breathe and wake up. He knew she wouldn't, of course, his senses and mind told him so, anyone with half a brain would know, but he refused it.

Dazedly, Harry stared as around him Aurors started their search for clues. The door flew open, and in walked Snape, robe billowing as always. Then Snape's eyes locked on the girl's face, and Harry saw for a moment the undiluted horror reflected in the eyes of the man.

'A Slytherin then,' Harry realized. But before he could think about it, Snape had walked over, gripping the front of Harry's bathrobe and pushing him against the wall. Nose to nose, Harry could smell Snape's breath.

"What have you done, Potter?" Barely constrained fury dripping from his words, Snape glinted at him.

"Speak! What did you... I will see to it, I..." Their eyes locked, and suddenly, Harry felt the world drifting from him. Memories floated to the front of his mind – of sitting at a table, writing something, of the letter to Sirius, of a smiling Greengrass, of reading a book with a heavy leather cover, of walking into the room, a chaotic mind needed an appropriate defence, he heard himself whisper... But just then, McGonagall interrupted, and Harry became aware of the bathroom once more.

"Severus, surely you don't suspect Mr. Potter! How could you possibly believe...!"

But Harry knew why, even with his head still hurting, and sure enough, even though he stepped away to escape the Aurors' glares, Snape hissed, "Oh, Minerva, you know like I do his hatred for rules. From his first day he overstepped boundaries, and you – like everyone else! – constantly looked away. He was bound to become a criminal, it's in his blood, Potter's spawn, isn't he? Just like his father!"

"Of whom he has no memories of, Severus!" McGonagall reminded him with a glare. "Mr. Potter is nothing like you see him! We are all distraught, but baseless accusations..."

But Snape's face contorted into the ugliest, most fitting grimace of insanity as he spat, "Distraught, yes, while he's laughing at us in his mind. He is insane and a killer, just like Black!"

"No," came the reply from the door. Dumbledore had arrived. All eyes locked on him, even the Aurors'. "Severus, if you'll allow...?" Turning to Harry, he asked, "Where were you today?"

"Quidditch practice. We started at one. I returned at six, showered. Went to dinner at quarter to seven. Then came here. It... Sir, it wasn't me, she was..." But Harry stopped and forced the images back, faintly aware of their hesitation to allow it, as if something was keeping them in place.

After another moment, Dumbledore turned to the room at large. "When have you seen the girl last?"

"The girl," Snape stormed, "had a name, Headmaster!"

Dumbledore nodded. "When have you seen Miss Brooks last?"

Frowning, Professor Sprout cleared her throat. "She left the Great Hall shortly after quarter to six. She hadn't eaten, I noticed, which is why I remember, and I was worried. Children shouldn't go on an empty stomach, and she looked... well..." Harry didn't miss the glances exchanged between the Aurors and Madam Pomfrey.

Dumbledore nodded. "So she left before Mr. Potter returned from Quidditch."

"He's lying, Headmaster, I'm sure," Snape protested, unwilling to let go of his suspicion.

"Well, until six, he does have quite a few witnesses. And we can always ask the portraits about him," Dumbledore pointed out.

Snape sneered. "Maybe they just didn't see him, Headmaster?" Harry felt anger bubbling in him. So Snape wanted to use the cloak against Harry?

Pomfrey cleared her throat. "While I'm no expert, I would still guess she died about seven or slightly later. Also, she didn't have an empty stomach at the time of her death. I found quite a bit of... Firewhiskey, mingled with water, of course."

Snape glared at her. "She was fourteen! My students don't drink at that age! They are well-mannered!"

"Well," one of the Aurors said, drawing attention to her, "this skirt," she held it up, "smells like Firewhiskey. The shirt too. And we found that one in the tub." She pointed to her partner, a balding, square-jawed man, who was holding an empty bottle.

"We'll just continue our investigation. How about an interview, now that the preliminary stages are finished?" The female Auror raised her eyebrow, daring the teachers to object, but Dumbledore nodded slowly.

Harry did his best to say everything he remembered. The already made bath, the lack of response, how he had literally stumbled into the body, had brought it up to the surface, gotten the word out (this earned him a strained smile from McGonagall). Finally, he told about the arrival of Madam Pomfrey.

The Auror turned to talk to the matron, trying to verify the story, when the doors flew open once more, and a tall redhead walked in. It took Harry a moment to notice the gleaming Prefect's badge on his robes, and another to see the green and silver tie.

"Sir," the boy began, addressing his Head of House, "I was told you were here. I have urgent business with you – one of the students... is..." He slowed down, realizing just what he had walked in on and who was present. The still nameless Prefect glanced at the girl, and for a second, his mouth fell open in surprise or shock. Then he swayed on the spot, staggered back, wide-eyed and speechless. But his lips still moved, framing some word.

Dumbledore stepped in front of him, blocking the view, and Harry wondered why he hadn't done so earlier.

"Mr. Summers," the Headmaster began in his best soothing voice. "You had something to report?"

The boy blinked, waking from his stupor and threw himself at Dumbledore, pushing him halfway aside with a wild shout somewhere between a name and a feral growl. But the Headmaster was surprisingly steadfast. "Mr. Summers, please calm yourself! I'm assuming you knew her?"

"I... yes, she... what happened?" the Prefect asked.

Snape stepped forward. "Mr. Summers, what can you tell us about her?"

The student blinked again. "I... we were..."

"Do you know how she could have ended up here?"

"I... yes, I... told her about it. We were..." He stopped, but his blush gave the complete answer.

Snape frowned.

"What happened?" the boy repeated.

"We're still investigating," Dumbledore replied. "Did you perhaps arrange a meeting tonight?"

The boy – Summers, Harry corrected in his mind – shook his head mutely.

Dumbledore sighed. "I am terribly sorry for your loss."

Snape stepped forward, pulling his student to the side. The gleam of gold drew Harry's attention, years of Quidditch having sharpened his senses. Snape had taken the badge. Then he turned to the room at large. "How do we proceed from here on out?"

Dumbledore again took control of the situation. "Ah, yes. Pomona, if you would be so kind and escort Mr. Summers to the Hospital Wing? I believe he might need attention later on. Severus, any objections?" Receiving a headshake, Dumbledore continued, "You will have to inform your students, naturally, and knowing this school... Minerva, Filius, I think you should, too, before the rumours start. I trust you will find adequate words." Each teacher nodded. "Now then, Mrs. Kent?"

The Auror sighed. "It looks like we'll be here for a while, but go ahead."

"Then, Minerva, please escort Mr. Potter to his house."

"Ah, come on! Just a peek," Tracey whined. "He'll never notice anyway. And don't forget, I let you borrow my dictionary."

"Not the same, and you know it," Daphne pointed out. "I was merely too lazy to fetch my own. And I only needed it for one translation."

"And I only need a quick look at your reasoning. Just a moment and you'll have it back."

"You honestly think Professor Snape won't notice?"

"Even if he does, he won't comment on it."

"And fail us both. He does require us to do our own work if nothing else. You signed up for it; you have to do your own work."

Millicent smiled from her place on the couch. "I'm just glad I don't have to bother with it anymore."

"It's actually very interesting, nothing like the past years," Daphne defended. "Two out of five Professors is not enough for a decent education, that's why many struggled with it."

"Professor Umbridge wasn't bad," Millicent argued loyally, having had the same argument in the past.

"Her regular lessons were," Daphne replied. "Without the massive extracurricular work and help from the upper years, I wouldn't have passed."

"Me neither," Tracey injected. "Without the madman and the werewolf Lupin, we wouldn't have learned anything."

"You still haven't," Daphne teased.

"Very funny."

"It is, isn't it?"

Tracey opened her mouth to reply when the wall to the common room opened. Professor Snape stepped in. Silence reigned, everyone too curious to talk. "Prefects," he called, "Get the other students." They left. "Mr. Boyd, until further notice, you will fill in as the fifth-year Prefect."

The rest of the house traipsed in, some still rubbing the sleep out of their eyes. As Snape watched them spread around the room, Daphne sat up straighter. The cold, observing looks he gave as well as his mask-like expression couldn't mean anything good. And after the assignment of Boyd as Prefect... did that mean...

"Mr. Summers will be rejoining you in the next few days. He is currently in the Hospital Wing and shall not be disturbed."

Daphne released a breath she didn't know she had been holding.

Professor Snape let his eyes wander the room as if searching for something. "Has anyone seen Miss Brooks since dinner?"

Ellen Jones, a fourth-year, spoke up. "Sir, she left a while ago. I ran into her in the corridor."

"When?" Snape inquired.

"Shortly after quarter to seven, I think. I was coming back from dinner, sir. She was heading to the Entrance Hall, perhaps she went outside? That halfbreed has Krup-pups which Abigail likes."

Professor Snape pursed his lips in dislike. "Anyone else?"

Ellen glanced around nervously. When nobody spoke up, she turned back to the teacher. "I can inform her of whatever it is once she is back, sir, if you want me to."

Professor Snape sighed, and in the eerie light of the dungeons he looked harsh. "That will not be necessary. Earlier this evening," he continued louder than before, and Daphne could have sworn she heard a slight crack in his voice, "Miss Brooks was found dead."

A gasp went through the room. A few of the younger students began sniffing. Daphne stared in shock. She had seen the girl just a few hours ago. How could Abigail have died? Slytherins didn't do that, they survived, they were smart, they...

Professor Snape's voice drew her from her thoughts. "The investigation is still ongoing. I urge anyone who has relevant information to step forward." Silence reigned once again while everyone processed what they had heard. Professor Snape encouraging people to help instead of hinder the authorities?

The assembled students waited for the inevitable. Finally, Jonathan Crowley voiced the question that was on their minds. "How did she die, sir?"

Professor Snape seemed to consider his words carefully. "She was found by a student on school premises." Ignoring the gasps, he continued, "The Headmaster believes them to be innocent." He paused, letting it sink in. "Another issue arose however: A bottle of Firewhiskey was discovered as well. Does anyone have an idea where she might have gotten it?"

No one dared to answer that question.

"Again, I urge anyone with information to step forward. Should I learn of anyone withholding knowledge about either matter, I will be very displeased. Furthermore, my door will always be open. I advise you to rest for the time being, and continue with your class work as before." He jerked around and left his shocked house behind.

"I don't believe it!" Tracey exclaimed. "She borrowed my notes last week! I can't believe it!"

All around agreement was muttered, and similar stories were voiced. The first and second years were huddled in a corner. Nobody seemed to want to follow Professor Snape's advice. How could they? Rest, while one of their own was dead?

"When do you think her parents will arrive?" someone asked.

"They'll probably inform her mother tonight," Jones said. She looked horribly pale, perhaps feeling guilty.

"What about her father?" a first-year asked.

Jones bit her lip. "Well... he died. Last June. Worked as a guard in the Ministry, you see? And when the Death Eaters infiltrated it..."

Silence spread as everyone thought about it, until someone from the group of seventh-years spoke up. "Well, I feel for her mother alright, losing both. But I'm wondering: Dying in school? Doesn't anyone else find that suspicious? Isn't the school supposed to be safe?"

Nobody answered.

Harry looked towards the stairs leading back to his dorm. It was still far too early to be up by far. If he snuck back upstairs, he might get a few precious hours of sleep before classes.

But he couldn't. After Professor McGonagall had escorted him to his house and had explained the situation, conveniently omitting Harry's part in the discovery, he had watched from a chair in the corner as his housemates discussed the new development. But after over an hour of fruitless talk, he had excused himself and had gone to bed. Only, sleep had evaded him. He had listened as Ron and Neville had come in, whispering among themselves about the expected retaliation of Slytherins and the necessary precautions to ensure the safety of the younger years.

But even after Neville had fallen asleep and Ron had begun snoring, Harry couldn't get to rest. Too many thoughts were keeping him awake, and he had trouble forgetting her face. If he had been just a bit faster, had arrived maybe ten minutes earlier, could she have survived? Or if he had used his head, he might have tried the spell he had seen Bill use at Grimmauld Place.

And so, after rolling around until after three, Harry had left the dorm. Normally, he would have worked on his Occlumency, but it was no use. He hadn't wanted to sit still, and he hadn't wanted to control his emotions. He had already learned to fill his mind and still keep working, creating a mindset he could operate with while hiding his true self behind inconsequential memories. After much deliberation, he had chosen a mix of overwhelmingly depressing and horribly painful thoughts in hope of driving every intruder out, if need be. And he had found to his great surprise that he had an easier time learning and remembering.

Since working on his Occlumency hadn't seemed appealing and he had felt the aversion to staying put, he had left Gryffindor House. Walking through the corridors, slipping to the Astronomy tower and the dungeons had felt good. Liberating, in a way. But as his need to be on the move had diminished, he had longed for the safety of his House leading to him standing in the Common Room, thinking about his course of action.

He glanced around the room. The elves had already cleaned up, apparently, as the mess from earlier had vanished. Having nothing better to do, Harry strolled over to the fireplace. Normally, he would have found it amusing to be alone in the quiet Common Room that was usually bursting with people scrambling to do their homework or noisily playing games. In the light of recent events, he instead took solace in the wide space and the soft glow of the embers.

On one of the tables, he saw a book. People usually forgot some of their things, but he found it to be a welcome distraction, and picked it up.

"Introduction to Runes," he read on the cover.

Curiously, Harry opened the book.

Two hours later, Hermione came down the stairs and found him reading.

"Morning, Harry," she greeted, surprise painting her voice, but whether because he was up or because he was reading, he couldn't figure out. "Slept well?"

"About as well as was to be expected, I guess. You?"

"Hardly, but then, I share a room with Parvati and Lavender. You know them, this is prime gossip material. I'm surprised they're not already up, pestering people. I planned to go to the library to do a bit of research for Arithmancy. I don't suppose I could convince you to join me?"

"No, thanks. You go have fun," Harry told her with a smile that didn't reach his eyes. Apparently, she noticed, as she frowned.

"Harry... do you want to talk?" She hesitated, gripping her robe to keep herself from wringing her hands. "I... I don't think anyone else made the connection, but... you came in with Professor McGonagall, and..."

He didn't need her to finish the sentence. Of course she would have picked up on it. "I don't want to talk about it. Don't take this the wrong way, but... I'd rather..."

"Wallow in pity, I get it. Harry, we're friends, aren't we? You can come to me, if you want, and I'll listen."

"Yes, I know. Maybe some other time. I'm trying to... look forward, not back, if that makes any sense."

She bit her lip. Oh, he could see how much she wanted to talk. But after a while, she nodded and forced herself to smile as unconvincingly as he had earlier. "So, what are you reading? Must be something really good if it's keeping you down here and away from bed."

Shrugging, he showed her the book. "Introduction to Runes. Someone forgot it, I picked it up."

Hermione raised an eyebrow. "Runes? I'm surprised, you never said anything. Mind you, I'm happy to see you applying yourself to more than just your usual coursework. Now all I have to do is corrupt Ron, and then we can be a well-educated group of friends, conversing about the secrets of magic instead of food." She grimaced, but he knew she was teasing him.

"It's not as bad as I imagined. You know, they really should tell us about these classes before we have to choose. How are we supposed to know which are good or bad? I mean, Runes might be useful for something..."

"Curse-breakers, Aurors, Unspeakables..." Hermione immediately began.

Harry shrugged. "Well, no use crying over spilt milk. Or, you know, wasted opportunities."

"Wasted opportunities? Something you want to tell me?" she asked with a raised eyebrow.

"Well, O.W.L.s, you know. We're N.E.W.T. students, so..."

But Hermione just shook her head wide-eyed. "There is nothing stopping you from getting a Runes O.W.L. but your own laziness."

"But I don't think I'll be able to fit Runes into my timetable, and especially since I'd have to join the third-years, so no. That'd be three years of learning in two years of school. I don't think that'll work out," he said.

"Harry," Hermione groaned and sat down in one of the armchairs, "the O.W.L.s have nothing to do with Hogwarts! The teachers have no authority over those exams; it's the Ministry workers you'd have to convince of your proficiency. And they don't check whether you took the classes, why should they?"

Harry blinked. "But every British witch or wizard goes to Hogwarts."

"They do, yes, but because it's seen as superior to the alternative." Seeing his blank stare, she elaborated. "We weren't ordered to go to Hogwarts, we were invited. Parents are allowed to send their children abroad, for example, but they can also hire a tutor. The Ministry sends examiners here because they can cover about ninety-nine percent of all examinees. But ultimately, no, Hogwarts has no say in the O.W.L.s."

"So why do we have to go to classes then?"

"That is a requirement in the school charter. If you want to do your O.W.L.s as a Hogwarts student, you have to sign up for the respective classes. Keep in mind, the school actually tells the Ministry which O.W.L.s we intend to sit. We don't have to sign any paperwork or applications. But then, we are perfectly allowed to go to the exams the Ministry sets up elsewhere. Of course, most of the time, no one shows up."

"That's why I had no worries dropping Muggle Studies – I'm Muggleborn, all I have to do to get the O.W.L. and later N.E.W.T. is sit the exams at the Ministry, and let's face it, those won't be a problem for me. It's also why you won't find a Wizarding Culture class, although the O.W.L. and N.E.W.T. exist."

"Wait a second. Muggle Studies is taught at Hogwarts, why not Wizarding Culture as well? If there's an O.W.L. for it..."

Hermione pursed her lips. "Well, purebloods deserve the chance to earn that O.W.L., don't you think? An O.W.L. to balance out Muggle Studies, which is a class Muggleborns have an advantage in? But they don't need to be taught about their own culture."

"Well, yeah, but don't Muggleborns deserve to get that Wizarding O.W.L. as well?" Harry asked. "Purebloods could sit the Muggle Studies classes if they wanted to. So shouldn't there be a Wizarding class as well then?"

"That would be a decision for the School Board; they'd have to sign off on it. The same School Board that consists of highly respected members of society, all hailing from wealthy – and more often than not old – pureblood families."

"Discrimination?" Harry frowned.

"Are you honestly surprised? Really? But it doesn't matter. There are far worse problems than that. But back to topic – you can still get a Runes O.W.L. All you have to do is learn the material and prove your competence."

"Err, alright, if you say so." He glanced at the book in his hands. "Runes, then. I've read a bit, but I don't think I could really get the hang of it."

"Well, it's different alright, yes, but not impossible. It's simply not magic as we are used to in our core classes."

Harry smiled. She obviously tried to bait him, perhaps to reaffirm her identity as a know-it-all. Well, if that was what it took to make her happy... "The book said something about a distinct approach setting the discipline apart?"

Hermione nodded at his interest. "Runes is very useful, but different in how it approaches a task. Transfiguration and Charms rely on the will of the caster. Runes are... well, rules, if you know what I mean. I can charm a rock to be warm, let's say to have heating in a house, but after a while, the charm will wear off, and I'd have to reapply it. But I could also create a runic layout that achieves the same result. Since the runes are permanently carved into the stone, they will keep the effect going until they are destroyed. All it requires are some runes describing the intended function, being warm in the example, and some runes drawing the necessary power."

Harry blinked at her. "Power? You... wait, you lost me there."

"Well, alright. The first set of runes would say: collect power. Energy. Magic. It doesn't really matter what you call it. The second set of runes says: Take the power, make the stone warm. Simple rules which create the intended effect, and as long as the runes aren't disturbed, it will work. That's why Runes is an important subject for curse-breakers. It is entirely possible to set a trap that will only stop working after the runes are disabled; let's say, if weight is applied to a set place, repulse it. With enough force, it is possible to smash someone headfirst into the ceiling. With enough force..."

"Splat," Harry finished. "I get it. So, Runes are based around that concept. If possible, do A. If B, then do C. Alright."

"Yes. And as you can imagine, there are a lot of possible uses for runic layouts. Permanent wards for houses, for example. Heating, like I said. Illusions, like the ceiling in the Great Hall. The paintings, of course. The staircases."

"And they don't wear off? They just keep going?"

"Until the runes are disturbed, yes, either by cutting off the power, or by destroying the functional components. Of course, any smart rune-carver will think of backups and safeties as well. There is also the possibility of linked runic layouts. It'd be something like a crossword, with some runes being part of more than one layout. That makes it difficult to disable certain parts because it would bring down more than just what is necessary or wanted."

"Alright, got it. Sounds like fun."

"It is. Granted, I prefer Arithmancy, but Runes is still a very interesting subject," Hermione replied.

"It looked difficult every time I saw you working on it."

"Far from it!" Hermione yelled, before, frowning, she added, "Well, I guess it is. But for any decent Muggle-raised witch or wizard, it is considerably easier. It is related to logic and combination, you see? That's why anyone with fundamental understanding of those has an easier time with Runes. The same can be said about Arithmancy. And that is perhaps the most important subject in existence, as it is essentially magical theory."

"Now I know why you love it. Theory, huh?" Harry asked with a smile.

"Yes. Knowing Arithmancy, one has a better understanding of Charms. Why does casting Lumos create light? Where does it come from? With Arithmancy, one can create better runic layouts, since we were talking about those."

"So you can write better rules?"

"Something like that, yes. Runes, unlike charms or direct transfigurations, are set once they are written. Arithmancy allows better rules. More precision, for example. Or, if more than one magical is working on something, easier cooperation. Actually, Arithmancy is especially useful for Runes, since those are, well, set in stone, and meant for centuries."

"Well, let's take..." She let her eyes wander around until they snapped back to him. "I know, let's take the stairs to the girls' dormitories. They are protected by a runic layout and only allow girls to go upstairs, right?" When he nodded, she shook her head. "No, they also allow teachers, for one. But let's go over the basic idea. So, to bar boys, the runic layout has to recognize the gender of whoever tries to go up, it has to have some form of reaction to boys trying and be powered somehow. Those are the three components necessary for the stairs.

"The stairs become a slide, remember? That will send boys back down. Also, the Head of House will be notified, but that's not relevant right now. But then, that will only work if the boys walk upstairs. What about someone trying to fly? With a broom? A slide won't bother him much. So, the runic layout has to include the case of someone walking upstairs as well as someone flying, by broom or carpet."

"Just out of curiosity, what happens if a boy tries to fly upstairs?"

Hermione glared at him, but after a while relented. "He'd be flung out. Very forcefully and spinning. And you will notice the different punishments in place? Simply trying to sneak up results in a shaming experience. Trying to outsmart the protection results in far more severe consequences."

"Then the lesson is to not try to be smart?"

"It is to not try to intentionally circumvent the protections," she corrected. "But back to topic. So we have something in place as an appropriate punishment. But then, the runic layout has to recognize the gender, right? And that is actually very difficult."

"Come on, you're kidding! That can't be hard, can it?"

"You doubt me?" Hermione challenged. "The ward protecting the stairs cannot have a loophole, otherwise someone might still slip through. It can't be as simple as checking for... physical attributes, obviously."

"Wait. Why not?" Harry interrupted. "That sounds reasonably easy. If invasive..."

"Well, what about Tonks?" Hermione asked. "Is Tonks male or female?"

Harry laughed out loud. "Tonks' a girl! A... a woman, I mean!"

"Is she? How would you know? Tonks can change her appearance at will. In fact, she can change pretty much everything about herself, and certainly well enough that a simple check for whatever is hidden under her clothes won't work. But you are right, Tonks is female since her core is flagged as such. But what about animagi? A fly animagus, for example? So, the runes have to identify the gender irrespective of form, and be able to distinguish between a fly and a wizard pretending to be one."

"Damn, there goes that idea," Harry mumbled. "Alright, so it is complicated, I get it."

"Yes, it is. Luckily, witches and wizards are easy to distinguish because of their core."

"'Tonks is female, since her core is flagged as such.' You said that, yes," Harry replied. "But you never said what you meant."

"Well, it means every witch and every wizard has a magical core which allows them to use magic. And each core has a gender assigned to it. This... label, if you will, useless, as far as anyone knows, is something on an axis from male to female. You'd be marked somewhere on the male part, and I on the female part. Easy enough, but unfortunately, some people don't play by the rules."

"They are on the wrong side?"

"They aren't on that axis. Goblins for example are identified as neither, but still have two ends of their own. People like Professor Flitwick would show up as neither, as well, and be distinguished as... I don't know, apples and carrots."

"Carrots?" Harry questioned with a raised eyebrow.

"Well, you get the picture, they aren't labelled as human female or human male, and the runic layout wouldn't know what to do with them. Depending on how it is written –'Keep all males out' or 'only let girls upstairs' – part–goblins might be allowed or not. Which would mean that for every possible mix of races you'd need a different copy of the rules – for Runes, it's a core issue. Keep carrots out, keep human boys out, keep boys with giant blood out... it'd result in a large list, but ultimately, it might still be possible, if it weren't for part-trolls, part-Veelas and similar mixes of certain degree."

She paused, enjoying the moment.

"See, they don't have different ends of an axis. For example, every part-troll with at least an eighth troll blood is recognized as male. They simply lack any distinction as far as their cores go, and are not only identified as humans, but as boys. Even the girliest of girls would still look to the runic set-up like a boy. Conversely, part-Veelas always show up as female. Goyle could be part-Veela."

"That'd be funny! So... Lavender could be labelled as male if she had troll-blood?"

"It doesn't have any meaning, you know? It's simply boys and girls being -1 and 1, respectively, none better than the other. Runic layouts are hard to write, since, to be good, one has to think of a lot of possibilities. After all, knowing how something is written means knowing its weaknesses. And keep in mind, these layouts aren't perfect. Teachers, even males, can go up, because, at the time when it was written, male teachers were around. However, male Prefects are still ejected, because the Prefect system hadn't been in place at the time, and the last two years had their responsibilities instead."

"So, how are the stairs protected then? You talked about what wouldn't work, but never how it was solved."

"Even if I knew – and I don't – I wouldn't tell you. Harry, you are a good friend and actually one of the most decent people I know, but you are still a boy," Hermione replied, rolling her eyes.

"Alright, I get it. Fine. Runes, then. They sound useful..."

"They are," Hermione agreed.

"... but I doubt I'll have time for that," Harry finished.

"You could always do it after we're finished with school, you know? That's when I'll get my Muggle Studies O.W.L., and you can bet on it, I will drag you there as well," she threatened.

"After school. Well, let's see," Harry answered. But his thoughts returned to his future. After school? He couldn't see a life after school, not for himself, actually.

He had found his purpose in life: ridding the world of Voldemort, dying in the process. It was poetic, he mused, as he sat back and stared into the fireplace. The Boy-Who-Lived, sacrificing himself for the world, possibly without anyone ever learning of the final act.

He'd let Hermione worry about O.W.L.s, Harry decided. It was her way, her life and destiny to grow into an adult, to live. Whereas she prepared for her life, he prepared for his death.

Funny how some things worked out.
