Chapter 21: The Truth Comes Out

All three went straight to Flitwick. Hermione explained, saying teachers were more likely to believe her. He called the Aurors, who listened, asked many questions, then left to investigate. Each student received ten points they felt they hadn't earned.

Day after day passed. Malfoy stayed. They watched him, and he had spotted them doing it. He knew what they had done, but didn't seem to mind.

Hermione began doubting the theory: surely the Aurors would have stopped him if he were guilty? Ron wavered. Harry didn't. The belief gave him a sense of accomplishment, one small part of his life under control.

At the end of January, Hermione returned Smith's book. She wanted to learn Legilimency too, but Lavender and Parvati were too curious to leave it lying around. She'd revisit it once she mastered Occlumency.

After dinner on the first February Sunday, talking about the publicized attack on a politician's family, they ran into Tonks. Harry still found her Auror robes odd.

"There you bunch of rascals are," she greeted them. "Great, saves me a lot of time searching for you. Wotcher, Harry. Hermione, Ron, everything alright?"

"Tonks," Harry interrupted her, "what have you done about Malfoy?"

She frowned at him. "That's not something I should be talking about, especially not to students."

"We pointed you in that direction," Harry protested. "We deserve to know what came of it. We aren't little kids."

Rolling her eyes, Tonks shook her head. "Fair enough. Well, we checked. We found no evidence to link him to the deaths, but you had assumed as much, hadn't you? And we also checked him. No Dark Mark."

"So he's clean?" Ron asked disbelievingly.

"Legally, yes. No evidence either way. He could still be working for Voldemort. Recruitment might be his trial for full membership, and I think you're right. But we can't give someone truth serum on suspicions." She looked around. "Leave it to the grown-ups now. Mad-eye was happy with how you handled it, by the way. Said you weren't completely useless. High praise from him."

"Moody wants revenge for his imprisonment," Tonks added. "I pity Pettigrew. But you lot should head to your common room." As they turned, she stopped them. "Nearly forgot. Dumbledore asked me to give you this, Harry." She handed him a scroll.

Walking up the stone steps, Harry quickly unrolled the letter.

"Another lesson?" Ron wondered. "I thought you needed that memory from Slughorn before you could continue."

Harry tried to shrug carelessly. "Well, yes. This is something different. I asked Dumbledore for help with a small issue. We'll meet next Saturday."

He had thought he had done well, but Hermione seemed to have picked up something. "And what is it about?"

"Oh, just a question that came up," he told her, avoiding her gaze by giving the Fat Lady the password. Stepping through, he saw the tables in the common room already occupied. "Oh, hey," he yelled at Dean and Seamus, "how about a game?" He nodded at the cards in front of them.

"Err, Hermione," Ron tried, his voice as sweet as he could, "about that essay for Flitwick, think you could..."

"Bring it here," she sighed.

The next week, Snape assigned an essay even Hermione struggled to understand. In Herbology, Jumping Vine seedlings slipped through gaps in their protective gear and clawed at them. Neville loved them; Hermione grew to hate them. Slughorn kept fleeing Harry, and the Prince kept Hermione grumbling. Meanwhile, Harry pushed to finish his homework early. Ron complained about lost games, Hermione seemed suspicious, but neither stopped him.

On Saturday after lunch, Harry sat finishing his last essay. Everything else was done. His mind wasn't on the work. Maybe he had been too hasty? There was no dry run for the procedure. If it failed, there might be no Harry Potter in a few hours. Even if it succeeded, he wouldn't be quite the same. He checked his watch. Just over an hour. Would he meet his parents, Sirius? Would they be angry? He wasn't trying to die. Quite the contrary.

"Ready?" Ron interrupted his thoughts.

"Yeah. Not great, but it should do fine enough," Harry replied, rolling up the parchment.

"You're mental, you know that, right? You have the whole weekend to deal with this stuff and you force yourself to finish over a day early."

"He's not mental, Ron," Hermione chastised him. "For once he's showing a proper work ethic, even if it is to balance out his dreadful grasp of the theory."

"Same difference. Listen, Dean and Seamus want revenge for last time, so I'll join them, but... can you lend me some money? You know I'm good, I just don't have any cash on me."

Harry handed him some coins. "Just don't lose too much." He watched Ron hurry off. Part of him wished life was that simple.

"Harry?" Hermione asked, her voice quiet. With a wave of her wand, she cast some privacy charms. Harry noticed mainly because the volume of the room lowered and for a second the air had shimmered.

"Yeah?"

"This business you are dealing with later..." She bit her lip. "I've watched you. I know you. You've kept it secret from us, and it worries me."

"I merely didn't mention it beforehand," he tried, but even to him it sounded feeble.

"It isn't the first time. You only told me about Greengrass because I overheard Ron. You didn't write, you didn't tell me when you returned, and that was enormous. Now you've spent a week avoiding questions about Dumbledore." She looked at him. "I thought you didn't trust me. But I know how you think. I'm starting to suspect another reason. You said Slughorn's memory must be important because he hides it. Well, so must this. I think you're trying to protect us from pain. But when you don't tell us, I worry about what you might do."

He looked at her. "I'm sorry about the contract. It wasn't something to blurt out or write in a letter. I wanted a quiet moment to explain." She smiled. "Tonight, I doubt we'll leave his office. There's a health issue from Voldemort's attack. It hasn't hurt me so far, but we want to make sure it stays that way. Dumbledore worked out what to do. Since it's linked to Voldemort, we don't want anyone else involved."

"Why Professor Dumbledore and not Madam Pomfrey?" she asked immediately. "She can be trusted and has extensive knowledge as well."

"No one else survived that curse. This is unique, highly obscure magic. Even Madam Pomfrey would likely be out of her depth. Besides, imagine the world hearing the Boy-Who-Lived had something worse than a cold. They'd start my obituary. Strange year. Inheritance, marriage..."

"About that." Hermione raised an eyebrow. "You and Neville went to Gringotts last summer. Wouldn't they mention the contract? Greengrass turned seventeen before Christmas, a hundred-and-forty-four days after late July. She'd just received her letter when you happened to have family business."

Harry bit his lip. "A letter saying a Second Rank existed, not my name. I still wouldn't get mine until March."

"It's suspicious, Harry. You keep things from us for stupid, noble reasons. We want to help, but can't if you leave us in the dark."

He sighed. "Suspicious it is, then. Should've guessed. Well, I'll give you the brief overview. But you can't tell anyone, not even Ron."

"But..."

"No one, not even Ron," Harry insisted. She reluctantly nodded. "Neville learned of it in summer and told me. We met Greengrass, but she wanted me to dissolve the contract and no one could sign for the Blacks. In November she asked again. She didn't want Malfoy. Thought him unpleasant – she had a hunch even then! So we planned this contract. Seven years, with a back door."

"You planned for that?" she gaped at him. "Are you... you are completely mental!"

"On December nineteenth, 2004, House Greengrass pays House Black some gold, most assuredly not as compensation for a marriage. If Daphne's alive, a lot more. Separate vaults, so she can't touch my wealth. Little contact, so nobody thinks she's allied with me. She's distressed, naturally, marrying 'that Potter', but accepting tradition. Most decidedly not an act." He paused. "She gets free of Malfoy, House Black gains wealth, he gets nothing for our enemies. A business deal to keep her alive."

"And you had planned it for weeks without telling either Ron or me?" she asked, hurt.

"The fewer people know, the less likely it is to get out. I trust you, but it wasn't just my secret. Her safety had to come first. I'm sorry, but..."

"Yes, I know. I can imagine. You are mad to have considered it in the first place, Harry, and I'm very unhappy about it." She shook her head. "Back to this health issue, then."

Harry looked toward the window. She had cornered him. "It's a spell misfire that hasn't been fixed. Maybe it would never cause trouble, but I don't want to risk it. If all goes according to plan..."

"Don't say that!" she told him, a fierce look in her eyes.

"If all goes according to plan, I'll be fine tonight. It shouldn't take long. If not, recovery might take a while. I'm willing to deal with the consequences."

"But why didn't you tell us in the first place? We would have wanted to be there, I'm sure Ron would agree with me!"

"I know. But the presence of others might not be best during the procedure. I'll tell you everything I can when I return. Trust Dumbledore. I heard he's quite a talented wizard."

Hermione snorted. "That he is."

"And trust you, because you would never lie to me? Bollocks, and you know it. You're doing something reckless, and that's why you kept it secret."

Harry smiled. "There is a risk, but sometimes risks are inevitable. I'd like it dealt with before I face Voldemort."

"Someone else might deal with Voldemort," Hermione argued. "You don't have to."

"Someone with twin cores, his undivided attention, and a way past Bellatrix? It has to be me, Hermione. The prophecy..."

"It says one must die by the other's hand. You could slip him a potion, send a bomb or something."

"A bomb?" Harry chuckled. "Really? A bomb?"

"Why not? An envelope with an Undetectable Extension Charm, the bomb inside, and when he opens it..." She mimed an explosion.

"I doubt he gets much mail. Pink hearts? 'My Dearest Dark Lord'?" Harry shook his head.

"We could make it look like something else," she tried.

"He's a half-blood, not stupid. He'd suspect a trap, and if he survived, he'd have another weapon. Clever idea, though. How did you think of it?"

Blushing, she avoided his gaze. "Well, I thought about cartoons. You know, sending each other bombs, tricking the other and similar plans."

Harry shook his head. "Well, as nice as that idea is, I think I should leave. It's better to be a bit early than too late."

"See you soon, then," she replied with a smile, dropping the privacy spells.

He stared after her for a moment. Then he sighed and whispered, "Goodbye, Hermione." He turned and left through the portrait hole, knowing what he had to do and wishing more than ever to stay with his friends.

He hadn't noticed how far he had walked until he stood in front of the stone gargoyle guarding the staircase to the Headmaster's office. He gave the password and watched the guardian step aside. Magic was still very fascinating.

At the top, he knocked on the door, and, after being told to, entered.

"Harry, very good. How are you?"

"Fine, sir." Harry looked around. He knew the office too well. "How are you?"

"Equally fine, thank you. I revised your work, but couldn't eliminate the danger."

"I am willing to take the risk, sir. I trust your magical capabilities and knowledge."

"Kind of you, but we must prepare for the worst. If it works, you remain Harry Potter in the eyes of the law, responsible for past actions."

"Including contracts?" Harry inquired. He hadn't thought much about that before, but figured it wouldn't matter either way.

"Yes. You retain everything. If we fail, your possessions pass to your heirs."

"I have a will. Hermione checked it. She gets the books, Ron my Firebolt. The wealth goes to good causes, none of them Death Eaters, and Kreacher to the school to keep him in check."

"Very wise. Now then, Harry, are you ready?"

He nodded. "I suggested it, sir. Yes, I am ready."

"Sit down. Some sources mention fainting. Concentrate on your own being. Collect your essence, what defines you, and protect it with all your might. I'll create several bridges to speed the process. It will be uncomfortable, but I can't help with that, or the conflict once the entities meet. I can only create the paths. Once they exist, I cannot break them."

"I know, sir. It's about as much my theory as it is yours," Harry replied with a roll of his eyes and sat down on a wooden chair. Now that the actual procedure was upon him, he felt nervous.

"Yes, very well. I would also ask you to hand over your wand," Dumbledore said. "You won't need it, but I cannot risk you using it in your confusion."

Harry did as he was told and placed it on the Headmaster's desk. "Sir, if... if it fails, will you tell Hermione I'm sorry? She figured part of it out, and I told her I would be safe."

"I understand, Harry. Now then, are you ready?"

Harry took a deep breath and focused on himself. His lessons in Occlumency paid off, and he soon had good control over himself. He nodded. Behind him, he heard the rustle of sleeves, and the anxiety doubled.

And then, it felt as if icy spikes were driven into his head. They seemed to split, as if barbs grew on them, themselves plunging into his mind relentlessly. His own being seemed to be pushed aside; with every passing second, the pressure increased. Surely his head would explode soon, flashes of his memories bubbled to his forehead, but he had to keep it together, couldn't let the other one win, had to come out on top. With all his might, he tried to concentrate on himself, blocking out the pain, blocking out the strange sensation. For no longer were there barbs and spikes, but instead it felt as if he was torn apart by a ravaging beast; sharp teeth dug into his mind, poisonous, it seemed like, for whatever they bit, it felt oddly detached, numb, or sometimes drifting away. The pieces seemed to be corrupted, slowly leaving his control. But he had to keep it together, couldn't let the other one win, he had to...

But it had lessened to... stinging... sleep, but not so close... felt the last of himself go, but maybe he would see his... mother again who had died for him... and then, he might finally... rest.

The mouth opened, not quite his own, but someone's, and it croaked words neither understood.

Dumbledore jerked at the sound. He had built the bridges against his better judgement and been shocked by the flood of Voldemort's soul. Had they miscalculated? Perhaps there had only been three pieces, with every other Horcrux gone? Too late. He had guarded his student for hours, watching the violent jerks, hearing screams and hoarse grunts. Then the fits stopped. Harry – or Tom? – had panted and tensed, eerily calm, before going limp in the chair. Dumbledore had called his name, hoping there was still a mind in there.

Now life returned. An arm swung, eyes fluttered. Dumbledore still had to know which side had won. Why hadn't he called Alastor? The Auror would have given them both an unforgettable lecture, but Dumbledore wished he had arranged a conveniently timed visit.

His student slowly shifted, sitting upright.

"How long did it take?" he asked, glancing to the window and the darkness outside.

"About three hours. How are you feeling?" Dumbledore inquired, still unsure.

"Just... tired, Professor." He struggled to his feet. "That was rougher than expected. You wouldn't happen to have a Headache Remedy lying around, would you, Professor?" He coughed, and fought to smile. "Because I could really use one right now." He looked over to the Headmaster. "You know... it's..." he broke off mid-sentence, as if frozen in his movement his hand raised halfway. Emotions flickered over his face, too fast for Dumbledore to read them.

Then the boy smiled, staring off into the distance. "It worked," he whispered. He chuckled slightly. "It worked," he repeated, and his smile grew into a grin. His chuckles turned into giggling.

Relief washed over Dumbledore. For hours he had feared he had killed the boy. What if Harry hadn't prepared properly? He should have checked. But the boy had returned. Survived. Apparently recovering.

The grin shifted ever so slightly, and a gleam appeared in Harry's eyes. And the giggles turned into laughter. But it was not the full-bellied laughter of Horace Slughorn or perhaps Hagrid. It sounded oddly disconcerting in the otherwise empty room, the paintings watching the incident with rapt attention.

And Dumbledore was shocked to see the student he had believed to be Harry throwing his head back and cackling, a mad glint in the eyes, very unbecoming for a sixteen-year-old boy -one who spread his arms slightly and continued to laugh hysterically. Instinct taking over, Dumbledore pulled his wand, cursing himself for not calling Alastor or really someone else. Even young Nymphadora would have probably been a valuable asset in the fight the Headmaster feared was looming. With a quick jab, he summoned Harry's wand from the desk, thankful for the slight advantage he had going into the battle with his student. But he was willing to do what needed to be done, and it had been in part his fault this new threat had surfaced. It was also his responsibility to take care of it, just as he had been forced to with Gellert.

He decided upon a classic opener, a stunner, and was about to cast it when he hesitated. Something was not right. The boy he had assumed to be Harry was still cackling, but his face looked no longer contorted with glee, but strained. The glint was still there, but more fearful than happy. The breathing was ragged, as if every bit of air was forced out of him. And the bold posture from before seemed tense. Before Dumbledore could make up his mind, the boy swayed dangerously. With a grimace, his breath hitched in his throat, he staggered, fought to stay upright. He managed it, looking off into space. Then, he fell over backwards without any life in him.
