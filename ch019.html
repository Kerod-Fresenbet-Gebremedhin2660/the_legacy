Chapter 19: Hoping for the Best

Thursday evening, Daphne stared at the parchment in front of her. But try as she might, she couldn't think of a useful application of the theory of Scrider and Dunne. Who honestly cared one bit about those two, really? The standard runes worked just fine, why alter them?

So Astoria had found herself a sweetheart. Lovely. If he kept the hell spawn from interfering, why complain? But now Astoria grinned as if having someone to snog meant she had outdone Daphne, stuck with Potter. Daphne only had to endure the marriage for a while. After that, she'd be free.

Potter had kept away, as he should. They weren't friends, and being seen together would be suspicious. But Granger had given Daphne looks somewhere between pity, sympathy and outrage. She had told her own dorm mates, so she could hardly object. Finnigan had begun smirking whenever they met. Jerk.

Did the teachers know? Professor Snape had been especially nasty to Potter, even demanding an unknown spell non-verbally. Watching him duel Draco had been equally amusing. Draco had won, if it counted when Snape had stuck Potter's shoes to the ground to stop him dodging.

Daphne enjoyed Potter getting into trouble for no reason. But Snape had started treating her worse as well, and that she did mind. She hadn't done anything different, but suddenly her Head of House punished her for speaking out of turn. Was that how the rest of the school felt? The day before, he had stopped. Potter hadn't jumped to her rescue as the professor seemed to hope, so he had switched to indifference.

"Stupid essay," she grumbled. Looking around the common room, she spotted Pansy with Draco in a corner. Luck was on her side. She rose and walked over.

"... isn't it?" he asked with a slight shrug. "But what can..." he stopped mid-sentence when he noticed Daphne stepping up.

"Daphne! How nice of you to join us!" Pansy said. "We were just talking about the summer. We thought we might organize a little get-together somewhere. Holidays from the family if you know what I mean?" She tittered.

Draco smiled lightly. "You might enjoy being away from them, wouldn't you?"

Nodding, she changed the subject. "Hello, Draco. Pansy, I wanted to ask whether I could borrow your notes from Runes. I'm stuck on that essay and can't think of anything."

"Oh, yes," the other girl replied. "I can imagine. Well, I've got them here, wait a moment." She began digging through her bag.

"So, have you enjoyed your time back? I guess it must be odd to be back, but changed fundamentally," Draco asked her.

Daphne glanced at him. "I'm the same I was before the holidays. It was nice to be allowed to use spells outside of school, but other than that..."

"You know what I mean," he countered with a chuckle and a meaningful glance.

"I don't see how that would change me in any way. It's not what I would have preferred..."

"I'm honoured," Draco told her, bowing slightly.

"Don't flatter yourself. It's not what I would have preferred, but it is also not the end of the world. I'm a pureblood, Draco, just like you. I don't enjoy it, no, but I will have to make do and hope for the best. It's simple as that."

"Hope for the best..." he mused. "That is very wise, Daphne. So, what best are you hoping for, then? The best for your new family or your old?"

She raised an eyebrow. "Not that it matters any to you..."

"I'm a Black too," Draco pointed out. "I have a vested interest in the survival of that family."

Daphne rolled her eyes. Trust him to pull that card. "Your care for your mother's family is charming. To answer your question, I am hoping for the best for myself."

"Ah, good answer. I like that, yes. Working for yourself, having your own goals in mind."

"I'm a Slytherin and a pureblood, Draco, don't forget that." Daphne smirked. Anyone listening would hear the pureblood daughter adapting to her situation. As long as she didn't slip up and Potter kept quiet, she'd be reasonably safe. "Hopefully, I'll end up on top."

"Oh," Pansy said, grinning with a quick side-glance to Draco, "ending up on top, I like the sound of that!"

Daphne saw Draco's cringe out of the corner of her eye and had to keep herself from laughing. She could see how that thought would displease him – Draco liked being in control, probably something he had learned from his father.

"Yes, Pansy, I can imagine," Daphne managed calmly. She hoped it would work out between them. Whatever people thought, Pansy was her friend; if she liked being with Draco, Daphne would support her.

"Well, here they are. Runes. You might want to have a look at that book there," Pansy indicated a comment on the parchment, "it is not exactly what we are looking at right now, but it has some comparable ideas. And knowing Professor Babbling like I do, she might just bring that up later in the year."

"All right," Daphne said with a nod. "Thank you. I'll leave it on your night stand later."

Daphne settled back into her seat. Trust Pansy to find Runes interesting. Her aunt, Mrs. Ambers, restored enchanted objects at an antiques store. Boring work, but lucrative, and Pansy respected her success even if she never planned to work herself.

As Daphne looked over to her friends, she noticed something. Pansy and Draco had glanced her way, and neither had looked too happy. Odd.

But she focused back on her work.

Sunday began clear and frosty. Daphne didn't mind winter, mostly because warming charms and fireplaces made it pleasant. She could imagine cuddling with a future lover in front of a fire. Summer was still better: swimming, holidays, sun and sundaes. As soon as she found herself a lover, there would be uses for him there too.

Her homework done, she had slept in, eaten breakfast and gone for a stroll. Even after five years, the castle could surprise her. Once she had found a drawing room with dusty drawers and moth-eaten robes, another time a broom closet behind an artistically carved door. A painting had taught her some choice swears. And there was Chuck, the gossiping boy in the third-floor mirror.

Her parents planned to surprise Astoria in Hogsmeade after Valentine's Day, close to her birthday. Maybe Daphne should warn them about her new boyfriend. It would give them something besides Daphne's marriage to worry about. How often did she need reminding to keep her head down? Yes, the Dark Lord had eyes everywhere. She wasn't a small child anymore.

She turned right, stopping at an ornate window of a drunken troll. Who put something like that in a school? Through one foot, she saw Hagrid walking with Potter toward the Forbidden Forest.

Should she hope the half-giant accidentally killed him? She'd be free earlier. But even she knew he wasn't as dangerous as Draco claimed. In fourth year her house mates had struggled to list the dangers in his classes. A Flobberworm bite, really? Millicent had given her a less excited account of the hippogriff as well: Draco had ignored instructions and got hurt. That sounded more like him.

They paused at the forest, then walked in. Potter had to be suicidal. Stay away from the forest, the Headmaster said every year. Hagrid was unlikely to hurt him, but would he see the danger in a beast that couldn't harm a half-giant? Daphne turned away. It wasn't her job to keep Potter alive.

Maybe she should send Great-Grandaunt Lydia a letter. Old as she was, she had moved to Spain with a gold-digger to spend her last years. But while he hoped to inherit all her wealth, it had been no secret in the family that Lydia had already spent everything. She had donated it, in fact – part of it for dubious causes, and part for St. Mungo's. They had cared for her son Abraham from the day he had been brought to them until his dying breath, mumbling about the enemies lurking in the walls.

Daphne shook her head, trying to rid herself of that reminder. She was too distantly related to him, she told herself. She was healthy. She wouldn't...

Summer. Yes, summer was fun, she thought hastily. Tanned guys, nice to look at. Water, sundaes, a cool breeze. Yes, yes.

The sound of voices pulled her from her thoughts. It sounded like they were coming from around the corner. Daphne made to turn around, but stopped when the higher of the voices sounded again.

"Well, I told her to shut it," Pansy spat. "What does she know, after all? Really, from the way the teachers are acting, you'd think they are..." She broke off in a huff.

Curiosity getting the better of her, Daphne walked closer.

"Well, you don't have to tell me," Draco said evenly. "But then, just because the current state isn't ideal doesn't mean it will stay that way. If you really want it to change..."

"I do, Draco, I do," Pansy sighed.

"Well, you might just have to do something about it, then. Change doesn't happen without someone initiating it."

"But why are the teachers so... ?"

Draco snorted. "Really, Pansy? Of course they try to help them; of course they have to be encouraged. What does she know, you ask? Nothing, and that's it. Without this special care, they wouldn't find their place in our world, and wouldn't that just be horrible?"

Pansy giggled quietly, and Daphne had to roll her eyes. She had seen her friend giggle before; she knew it looked more like she tried to sneeze onto her hand. "Oh, what a tragedy! Little lost Mudbloods."

"Muggleborns, Pansy. Muggleborns," Draco reminded her sternly.

Daphne almost tripped at hearing that. Draco Malfoy lecturing others on the politically approved terminology? What had the world come to? Maybe having his father imprisoned had removed the attitude from him? Daphne thought it might be an improvement, but she wasn't one to hope for miracles.

"No, the teachers have the right idea," he continued. "If the Muggleborns are ever meant to find their place in our society..." He left the sentence hanging, but from Pansy's renewed giggle, he had done something funny.

"Oh, Draco, you have no idea how much I missed you," she sighed. Daphne screwed up her face. She really didn't want to hear that, and made to leave the two behind.

"I was busy, I had a lot to do," Draco said. "I have responsibilities, Pansy, and I do take them seriously. This is a critical moment in my life." With happiness in his voice he continued, "I help shape a better world for wizardkind."

Daphne blinked. Where had that come from?

The following week, Daphne paid close attention to Draco.

Lying in bed on Friday, Daphne wondered how she had missed it. Initially she had dismissed the overheard conversation as Draco boasting to impress Pansy. But she had noticed his cruelty in November; it had troubled her enough to arrange a marriage to Potter. Millicent had seen it too.

He no longer flaunted his father's influence. Lucius was in Azkaban, true, but Draco wasn't behaving like the boy she knew. Could she trust her experience with him?

His civility over the holidays had made her think her fears unfounded. More than once she had scolded herself for arranging her betrothal to Potter. Had she really thought Draco worked for the Dark Lord and would hand him all the gold he could?

But the conversation had reawakened her suspicions. She needed to know whom she could trust. That had been the start of her troubles.

Draco talked to students from N.E.W.T. level down to third year. He tutored them, but often in quiet corners, glancing around to make sure nobody listened. Every now and then he smirked.

He was a diligent student. But when Snape taught counters to nasty curses, Draco smirked behind his hands. He did the same when Granger answered in class. He used to scoff at her. Why the change?

And the people he spoke to had begun keeping away. Nott was an acquaintance, so she hadn't missed him, or Baker or Furlong. But Pansy had progressively less time for her too. Even when Draco was absent, she had urgent business elsewhere.

The same people stopped talking when Daphne approached. She had asked Millicent and Tracey, but neither claimed to know why.

She twisted, feeling a bruise from Defence. It had been a normal lesson: Theodore and Tracey took turns attacking Potter instead of fighting each other in their three-way stand-off, until Snape disarmed him for doing too well. Daphne had partnered with Terry Boot and Susan Bones. When a misaimed disarming spell from Weasley sent Bones after her wand, Daphne had looked around. Boot watched Bones' backside, but Daphne noticed something else.

Snape was talking to Draco, apparently giving pointers. Nothing unusual for a Slytherin. Then her own duel resumed.

She hadn't remembered until that evening, under the shower. After the long day, she enjoyed the water and hummed a tune. Her dorm mates said she couldn't hold one to save her life. Probably true, but she had never cared.

Rinsing shampoo from her hair, she noticed the spot on her hand. Tracey had spilled water-resistant ink over her homework and shirt. Most people would take the tiny spot for a birthmark, but that only made it more annoying.

She scrubbed again. She could spell it away, but using magic for such a simple task felt like cheating.

It wouldn't come off. She considered Pansy's fingerless gloves, then dismissed them. Suddenly wearing gloves would only draw attention.

Then it clicked. A mark on the skin that couldn't be removed. Malfoy talking to Snape. The pieces fell into place.

Draco Malfoy was a Death Eater.

In a trance, she had returned to her room, put on her nightdress and got into bed. His father and aunt were Death Eaters; Draco had always discriminated. He fitted the description.

His indifference to her betrothal made sense. If he had joined the Dark Lord, he might know what was coming. What mattered a temporary loss to Potter, if he could watch his rival suffer later?

But would a Malfoy take the Mark? His name was an argument both ways: sympathetic to the cause, but hardly eager to take orders. Something was still missing. The cruelty could have been a slip. Everyone had been on edge in November; even Daphne had had nightmares again.

Most people would feel reassured. Draco wasn't a heartless criminal, he had merely cracked for a moment. But after her first relief, the fears resurfaced, and with them the images.

Before she knew what she was doing, Daphne cast protective spells around her bed. Even if Draco was perfectly normal, she felt horribly exposed. She didn't need a repeat of third year, with Sirius Black at large and her troubled sleep. She needed to feel safe in her own dorm.

She pocketed her wand. The spells would keep others away. She was healthy, wasn't she? It had been a while since any relatives had mental problems. She had never had any herself. She was sane, only slightly paranoid. February was looming; maybe that was why she was nervous.

She looked up and stared right at a deranged looking girl over at the door.

Daphne jumped back, hit the bed and fell onto it. She might have screamed. Then her mind caught up. The girl in the mirror, half hidden by its frame, lay on a mirrored bed. The toothy grin wasn't there, the madness didn't shine from her eyes. But Daphne had seen it, just as with the Boggart in third year. This time Carrie hadn't enchanted the mirror, likely on Astoria's orders. It was only Daphne's mind playing a trick.

She concentrated on her breathing. She was healthy. She wasn't like her family. She was sane. Great-Granduncle Gregor had simply slipped, nothing more. She was sane. Breathe in. Her grandfather had only made a mistake, nothing more. Breathe out. Breathe in. Her father's sister Phyllis had had a nightmare, or maybe she had been a sleepwalker without anyone knowing, she hadn't meant it, couldn't have meant it. Breathe out. Don't think about it. Breathe in. Ophelia hadn't been odd at times, she'd just been playing. Breathe out. Breathe in. Daphne hadn't lost her mind, she was still sane, just a bit unsettled. Breathe out. Bit by bit, she pushed everything from her mind. She really should have expected it, Daphne thought, as her hand clutched the sleeves of her shirt. She had stayed strong through the unfortunate accident from the first week, hadn't she? McLaggen had simply slipped, nothing more.

After a while, her heart settled. She should get Dreamless Sleep Potion, she thought. But she needed her head clear. She couldn't cheat. Hadn't she just defeated her fears on her own? It had only been a trick of the light.

On Tuesday afternoon, Harry stared into the gaping maw of a Singing Amazonian Bog Daughter. It looked as innocent as it could, considering the deadly poisonous hairs in its mouth. For safety, Sprout had taught it everything but genuine lullabies. Harry hoped the N.E.W.T. students or Peeves had invented the lyrics about biting where the sun didn't shine. Then again, even professors needed fun.

As he watched the hairs wave, sweet scent all around, Harry had a sudden realization. He dropped the mouse he was holding. Fortunately for him, less so for the rodent, it landed in the plant's mouth. The hairs stunned it and the maw closed, buzzing quietly. But Harry didn't notice. At last he understood something of what had kept him on edge.

He bent over to Ron and Hermione who were both staring in disgust at the plant they had been working on.

"Great, Harry," she told him, nose wrinkled. "We weren't meant to feed it yet, now we'll never get it to show us the dance."

"Relax, Hermione," Ron said to her, nose wrinkled. "I'm sure those plants weren't meant to dance in the first place, only to hunt. It's likely something Professor Sprout found amusing to teach them."

"Oh, really, Ron," she hissed back, "Professor Sprout does take her job seriously..."

"While I welcome the vote of confidence," the dumpy teacher yelled, "I would prefer you paying attention to your plant, Miss Granger. The same goes for you, Mr. Potter."

Harry nodded dolefully with his friends. Then, once the teacher had turned away, he bent over to them again. "Malfoy's a Death Eater," he whispered.

"Wh... what?" Hermione stuttered out, desperately trying to keep her hair away from the plant's tentacles. "That's ridiculous! Harry, that's... Malfoy isn't a Death Eater! That's... not possible, simple as that."

Ron nodded. "He's nasty, but a Death Eater? He's a braggart hiding behind his daddy. Can you imagine him doing anything worse than usual?"

Hermione frowned and peered at the plant in front of them. "Well, I wouldn't say it like that, Ron, he might have something to back up his words..."

"That's what I meant," Ron agreed instantly.

"But he doesn't talk about blood purity any more, Harry," Hermione said. "He turned over a new leaf. He has changed."

"But... I'm sure. It fits. He's the worst that could happen to Hogwarts, Death Eater Draco, so it's bound to happen some time."

"No, Harry. Every year you've faced some danger or conspiracy, but paranoia isn't evidence. A school year without lethal danger is too foreign for you. You see threats just to regain a sense of normalcy. Sometimes soldiers have trouble coping with peace too. I might write home for a book..."

"Miss Granger," Professor Sprout interrupted, "As much as I hate to do this, five points from Gryffindor. You are meant to work on the plants, not chat with your friends. Mr. Potter, Mr. Weasley?"

All three nodded and his friends returned to work. Hermione had a point; he did expect danger. That didn't mean there wasn't any. He knew he had the clues, but something was missing.

On Friday, Harry went to bed early. He still felt the late practices. He could have cancelled, but had to set an example, and he liked working with the team. Between classes, Dumbledore's lessons, duelling, Malfoy, Greengrass and the Horcrux, he had bitten off more than he could chew. Quidditch was different. Simple, something he controlled, with results he could see.

Stories would probably make Draco a tragic hero, Harry thought: loved, wealthy and well-connected, but led astray. The poor misguided boy, stuck between family and conscience. Idiots always loved their tragic heroes. If only Harry could convince Hermione and Ron!

He wanted to survive. Aunt Petunia would call it selfish, just as when he needed glasses or asked for a birthday cake and earned a week in the cupboard. But he wanted to grow up, get a job, marry again once the Greengrass deal ended, perhaps have children. Invite friends to boring dinners, drive Sirius' bike, spend a morning in bed. But the Horcrux remained. Until Voldemort died, Harry was bound by his destiny.

Two pieces of souls, he thought, snuggling into the blanket. If only there was another way. But dealing with Horcruxes meant deadly magic, deadly substances, deadly creatures. Great prospect. Yawning, he finally found his spot.

Body parts could be cut away. Or something stuck in him, like a sting or tooth, could be pulled out. He'd survived a Basilisk's tooth in his arm. If the fragment was in a trinket, he could drop it and let them destroy it. Or if they were talking about minds! Then they could have...

Harry was suddenly wide awake, staring unblinkingly into the distance.
