Chapter 18: Of Love and Contracts

Neville closed the book. "All right, that's that. Why did I let myself get talked into Charms?"

"Flitwick is nice?" Harry offered with a shrug.

"Half the time, I don't have any idea what I'm doing. With the way things are going... I'm just happy I dropped Defence. No more Professor Snape." He smiled lightly.

Ron grunted. "Should have done that, too, but no! Why did I continue it?"

"Because you didn't want to leave your friends at the mercy of a cranky crook?" Harry offered. "Really, Neville, you should be ashamed of yourself, now that you are gone, I have all his hate just for me. I feel really greedy, having no one to share it with."

Neville shrugged. "I can live with being a terrible friend as long as I don't have to endure him any longer."

Shaking his head, Ron narrowed his eyes. "Well, you're lucky you have a subject you love. The time you save with Herbology you can use for the rest. Me? Not so lucky."

"I put in more time for that subject than the rest of you, even Hermione. Most plants are part of some family so they aren't as random as you'd think. See, here," he pointed at Ron's homework, "you mixed up the sun-loving..."

"Don't care, Neville," Ron said with a yawn. "Potions, Herbology, Defence with Snape... Transfiguration. Why did I continue those?"

"Because your timetable would have been very empty if you hadn't," Harry said. "And you are doing reasonably well in Charms."

"If I had nothing else to do, yeah, I'd be good, but with all this other stuff... and Quidditch, you know, it does take up more time than I had thought."

"Well, Harry does schedule a lot of practices."

"Less practice than Angelina," Harry pointed out. "And less than Wood as well. But had I known how much time the Captaincy would cost me, I would have refused. I won't be here forever. Have you noticed how I've been taking Coote on the side occasionally?"

"I thought you had him do something," Ron wondered. "You want him to be Captain? What about Ginny?"

"She's a year behind us. She'd have one, maybe two years of Captaincy. I thought about recruiting someone really young, but... bad turnout for that, sadly."

"Since the try-outs? You've been thinking about that since the try-outs?" Ron stared at his best friend in disbelief. "Why didn't you say so? Why am I hearing about it for the first time months after the fact?"

"Since the end of November. I do have a responsibility to the team, don't I? There's just too much to do. My little side projects, classes, detentions, Quidditch –"

"Greengrass," Ron added with a frown.

Before he could speak up, another voice did, and one Harry had wanted to hear all evening. "What about Greengrass?" Hermione asked, stepping up to them. She held a book and some of her notes in her arms.

"Hermione!" Ron yelled in surprise. Some looked over, most didn't. Their corner was secluded enough to stay out of their hair.

Neville sent Hermione a smile and a nod.

"I hadn't noticed you," Harry told her, collecting some parchment and books to make a bit of room. She looked like normal to the inattentive observer, but he could see the red in the corner of her eye as well. "Are you alright?" he whispered, bent just right to block out the other boys. He wanted to leave her the choice of talking or not.

She smiled sadly at him. "I'll be fine. Just..." She broke off, shaking her head. "I missed you, all of you. And I couldn't hide any longer, not if there's work to do. Without my help, how are you meant to pass your tests?" She sat down in the free space. "But you haven't answered my question. What about Greengrass?"

Neville blinked. Ron bit his lip. Great friend, bringing it up but not daring to tell the whole story, Harry thought wryly. He threw up some privacy wards and was delighted to see Ron adding some of his own.

"Well, that's something I should have mentioned already, but didn't find the right moment for it. Greengrass – Daphne, that is – and I are kind of..."

She stared at him. "You and... Greengrass? Daphne Greengrass? From Slytherin? How did that happen? Since when?"

"Well, it's since Christmas, I guess," Harry replied. Not the whole truth, but not a complete lie either. Before Christmas, they had conspired, but hadn't been bound to each other. He was about to reveal the engagement when she spoke up again.

"But that is... ah, that explains a lot," she sighed, nodding. "So that's what happened. Ah, too bad. Well, you are still young, I guess, so it doesn't matter all that much. You still have time."

"Only a year younger than you, Hermione," Harry told her. "And what was that supposed to mean anyway?"

"It's just... shocking, that's all. I hardly know her, but... I have always thought, you know, you and Ginny..." Hermione shrugged half-heartedly. "But that explains why Ginny didn't go over her essay with you over the holidays. You were busy, then?"

"Wait," Harry interrupted, throwing up his hands. "Me and Ginny?" His voice rose in disbelief. "Ginny? Ron's little sister Ginny?"

Neville started laughing.

"Well yes," Hermione replied with a wave of her hand, "who else? You are still young, so I guess Greengrass might give you some experience for when you are ready for something more serious. But still... Greengrass, that is a shock – surprise! I wasn't aware you even knew her name."

"We were in Potions for years, it shouldn't be that surprising I know who is in class with me." Harry shook his head. "Why would I date Ginny specifically? Whatever gave you the idea that it'd be her?"

"All right," she replied, sounding slightly annoyed. "She had a crush on you in the past. She resembles your mother, and boys look for that. You share an interest – Quidditch. You are friends. Her family likes you. You saved her in our second year. You have similar nightmares in your past. You complement each other."

He ran a hand through his hair. "But that's idiotic! Half the reasons..." He breathed in, held it, then back out. "She loves Quidditch. I like it because I can fly and prove myself as something other than that Boy-Who-Lived people want to see me as. But there are dozens of girls who like the sport even in this very room."

"Oh, so a girl shouldn't be able to show you up?" Hermione asked with a raised eyebrow.

"Where did you get that from? No, let the girl show me up for all I care, but I'd prefer someone I can talk with, someone on the same wavelength. She loves the game, I like flying. Different, Hermione. You three are closer to me than Ginny. Luna might be as well, for that matter. Being friends does not mean a relationship upgrade is the natural conclusion. One doesn't necessarily look for the same qualities in friendship and love. No offence, Ron."

"She likes you," Hermione insisted.

"Even if she were the last woman on earth, she'd still be with Dean and off-limits. And if she's with Dean, she obviously decided against pursuing me. Dean is my friend. I'm happy for him. She's putting a lot of effort into the Quidditch team – he's giving her strength, I think. What kind of friend would I be if I resented him having a girlfriend?"

"And similar nightmares?" There it was again, the restlessness he had felt in the past. He stood up and started pacing.

"You've both suffered at the hands of Voldemort," Hermione reminded him.

"Ah, she lost her parents? I hadn't noticed. Grew up without family? The poor girl, she hides her pain well, I must say. She's hunted by him?"

"I meant –"

"I know what you meant, Hermione. I am hunted! I don't search for them, they target me. Me personally. I don't stand by my friends to help them in their danger, I am the reason for that danger. I cannot choose for myself; I am forced into this war. She got tricked; she paid the price and suffers for it, perhaps even for the rest of her life, yes, but she is not like me. If she so wanted, she could turn around, leave and live." He barely stopped himself from adding that he didn't have that option – his death was already an inevitable result of the war.

They were silent, Ron and Neville shifting uncomfortably. Harry held Hermione's gaze.

"I..." she began, "I didn't mean..."

"I'm sorry I was an arse, but... This is war, Hermione. My burden is not like hers. She doesn't have an army of bloodthirsty criminals after her. She'd feel bad, but she could walk away."

"It's our burden as well," Ron said, placing a hand on Harry's arm. "We chose to stay at your side. We won't desert you; we will go all the way with you, side by side. It is our burden too."

Harry wanted to laugh, wanted to tell them about his destiny. But it wouldn't be fair. Better to let them think it had been a cruel twist of fate. Looking at his pale-faced friends, he wished more than ever that he could escape. He didn't want to die. But only with the last Horcrux gone would the world be safe again. For a while, at least.

He forced himself back to the topic. "Even similar nightmares don't mean love is bound to grow. Others lost family as well. Am I meant to take it up with them too? And her childhood fantasies about the Boy-Who-Lived make me uncomfortable, as you know. Yes, I saved her, but mostly because no one else could have done anything in time. Everyone would have done the same, I think."

"I'd have called a teacher," Neville piped up. "Let them sort it out, maybe lend them my help and expertise."

"Well, everyone would have tried to do something, right? And my past deeds, they aren't that great. I did what I could, nothing more."

"It still might have left an impression, Harry," Hermione told him. "Call it childish, but it shows a deep loyalty to your friends. I doubt there is anything you wouldn't do to help us."

Ron nodded reluctantly. "Yeah, he'd die for us."

Harry laughed out loud. Perhaps Ron had Seer Blood in him, or Divination was merely stating the most disturbing possibilities and Fate planning the worst.

"Anyway, Ginny and I would clash, I think," Harry continued. "She'd tell me what to do and I'd be reluctant to give up the freedom I like to have. We make decent friends, but as a couple, I seriously doubt we could make it work. And just for the record, she doesn't resemble my mother."

"She's a redhead," Hermione reminded him, "just like your mother, Harry."

"If boys look for girls who resemble their mother, then I claim all girls grow up to become like their mother. And Molly Weasley does not look like my mother."

"Hey, what's that supposed to mean?" Ron shouted, whirling around.

"They have different body types?" Harry replied. "But there's something wrong with your theory, Hermione. It would have to be genetic to work in my case, right? I had no memories of her before first year, no photos, no description either."

"You... you knew nothing about her?" Hermione asked, unsure of herself.

"Come on. You have seen the Dursleys, haven't you? Do you honestly think they'd tell me bedtime stories about my parents? Or keep photos of them around?"

"But... you must have known," she claimed. "They must have told you about your parents. They are your family."

"Oh, they told me about them, yes," Harry laughed humourlessly. "But those were Dursley stories meant to teach me something about myself, not my parents; stories of how they got landed with me, and why I should be grateful they took me in out of the goodness of their hearts. Tales about the good-for-nothing drunks who got themselves killed, lazybones who didn't care enough about their child to stay alive. Stuff like that, essentially." Harry shrugged.

"But... but that's abuse! Verbal abuse, Harry!" Hermione screeched.

"So?" he countered with a raised eyebrow. "Even if it were, it doesn't matter. One summer with them and I'll be gone. I even considered not going back, but... Obligation," he offered with a shrug. "For over fifteen years, I have kept them safe. It's almost done, stopping now, so close to the end, feels as if I'd... quit or something. I might not have chosen that task myself, but I want to finish it. Once I'm out of there, I'll never see them again. They'll be happy, I'll be happy, everyone will be happy."

She stared at him, wide-eyed, perhaps wondering how she could have overlooked that fact about her friend. Ron looked anywhere but at Harry for fear of saying something stupid. It was probably also difficult for him to decide which side to take.

Finally, Neville restarted the conversation. "Well, Ginny and Harry, that wouldn't work well at all. Both are too stubborn to compromise on anything."

Hermione threw him a thankful smile. "Well, we'll see. You still have your whole life ahead of you, and the war will end one day. People change, Harry, and maybe you will discover something in Ginny you hadn't seen before. So for now, how about we shelve this conversation and look at the fun side of things?"

Ron nodded, smiling as well. "Sounds good."

"Right now, I want to know all about you and Greengrass. How did you get together?" Hermione gave Ron a surprised look. "You obviously knew, but you don't seem to have a problem with it. Your best friend is with a Slytherin!"

The redhead squirmed under her look. "Well, it wasn't his choice, was it?"

Hermione blinked, then grinned widely. "Oh, that's hilarious! So Miss Greengrass has sunk her claws into my best friend? Or, since she's a snake, her teeth perhaps? You do have it with strong, decisive girls, then, don't you? Does she have you call her something cute? Oh, and Valentine's Day is coming up..."

"It's not like that," Ron ground out, irritated. "It's a contract."

Hermione's chuckles stopped abruptly, as she froze mid-movement. She blinked, trying to figure out the meaning of the statement. "What do you mean, a contract?" When Ron blushed, but refused to speak up, she turned to Harry. "What does he mean?"

Harry glared at him. "Great going." But he had been about to tell her before they got side-tracked. He wasn't actively trying to hide it.

Turning to the other boys, he said, "Maybe we should go to the dorm?"

"Dean and Seamus are there," Ron pointed out. Neville nodded, but kept quiet.

"Why not? We could get Seamus and Neville up to speed. And Dean doesn't understand either, but I don't want to tiptoe around the matter in our own dorm."

Neville smiled at him. "I'll make sure they are decent, alright?" Receiving Harry's nod, he stood up and left for the stairs.

Together they collected their books, dispelled the privacy charms and went upstairs. Dean sat on his bed, with Neville picking clothes off the floor. A minute later, Seamus walked in from the bathroom.

"Alright then, everyone accounted for," Ron told the room, and both he and Harry cast privacy charms. Hermione watched closely, paying rapt attention to their wand movements.

"I'm surprised you haven't learned them already, Hermione," Harry said while pocketing his wand.

"I was busy with... my Charms project, you know?" she told him crossly, settling on Ron's bed. "It is surprisingly difficult. But don't you dare change topic now."

"Wouldn't dream of it," he sighed. "I chose to confide in you and expect you to keep the secret. That includes you as well, Dean, Seamus. In June, the title of Head of House Black passed to me. On my seventeenth birthday I will take control of the House, the vaults – everything, basically."

"That'll be a sweet seventeenth birthday," Seamus whistled. "The Blacks, as in Sirius Black?"

"As in Sirius Black, the innocently imprisoned, yes. On Christmas Day, I signed some papers as Acting Head of House as was my right. For a limited time, I took equally limited control over a donation. Only it activated a roughly hundred-year-old marriage contract with House Greengrass."

Hermione paled and jumped to her feet. "You mean... ? You can't mean...! That's barbaric! Harry! Please tell me you don't have anything to do with that!"

"She turned seventeen shortly before Christmas, which obligated her to fulfil the contract if a suitable candidate came along. When I signed those papers, for that briefest of moments I was considered old enough as well. Since both sides had a viable candidate, the contract activated. There was nothing I could have done afterwards."

"But it's all wrong! It's cruel, witches forced to marry against their will! The wizarding world is even more backwards than I had feared!" Hermione gripped her hair in outrage.

"So," Dean began. "So, Harry has to... what, exactly? I'm sorry, Muggle-raised, what's that all mean?"

Seamus took it upon himself, being closer to Dean than the others. "Well, it's part of the traditions – pureblood, of course – to strengthen the ties between families. Harry has to... err, marry Greengrass, apparently."

"That's it in a nutshell, yes," Harry agreed. "Quite a shock that evening when the owl arrived."

Hermione whirled around. "How can you just stand there and... and do nothing? It's a life that has been ruined! By the stupid traditions of dim-witted inbreds!"

Ron frowned, and Seamus jumped to his feet. "Oi! Those are me family ye're talking 'bout there!"

"As another pureblood," Neville said in a measured tone, "and one who has been taught these stupid traditions since he could walk and talk, I have to agree with Seamus. You are insulting our families there, and you do it based on prejudices."

"Pre... prejudices?" Hermione shrieked. "I do no such thing! But this is..."

"Mostly a thing of the past, Hermione," Harry interrupted. "I simply had my typical luck to stumble into one that hadn't been cleared away beforehand."

"So you're telling me," she began, "that purebloods sell their daughters like... like cattle! They sell their daughters to the highest bidder as breeding stock, and you knew about that, and you don't see anything wrong with that?"

Dean nodded slowly. "I'm sorry, but so far, I'm kind of on her side. That doesn't sound... well..."

Sighing, Harry took a step towards her. "Bill assisted me during the negotiations. He's more familiar with the traditions. He explained it to me."

"It's about alliances between families," Harry continued. "Back in the Feuding Ages, wizardkind thought in terms of clans or Houses. Sometimes they agreed to a marriage to strengthen an alliance. Other times it was to settle a feud – you don't fight family, you see? Neither side lost face. Ron, Seamus, Neville, you can help me, if you want."

"Naturally, a wedding needs a couple," Seamus threw in. "Sometimes they asked for volunteers, two who liked each other or were willing to do it for the family. Other times they brought every eligible member somewhere with barrels of ale for the night and checked the next morning where everyone came to rest."

"How do you know all that?" Dean asked the pureblood boys.

"Gran wanted me to know that stuff," Neville said. "She thought it was important to know where we came from, and my Grandfather was something of an expert."

"Sometimes there were no eligible candidates," Seamus spoke up. "The families could restrict who could be chosen. Only purebloods, for example, or no more than a number of years apart. If no one fit right away, they wrote general contracts. Should the requirements for both families be met, those who fit the bill had to marry. So there are contracts with specific people, Anna Apple and Bernard Berry, and contracts without anyone in mind. Promises, enforced by magic."

"And these contracts, what did they entail?" Hermione asked with a raised eyebrow.

"The general contract is replaced by a personalized one," Harry told her. "Dowries, clauses about not killing each other. In my case, the general contract demanded pure blood for the Greengrasses and no more than seven years apart. The personalized contract is as uncomplicated as they come. Neither of us was happy about it, so why make it more difficult?"

"That sounds a bit like marriage settlements in the Muggle world," Dean said with a frown.

"They are short if the families like each other," Seamus said. "But to stop a feud they came up with ridiculous demands to stall for time. Who got the house, who the cutlery? That's why it's tradition to have one family give a ram and one a ewe. Bride-groom and bride, only as animals, running around the wedding, eating and bleating. Pandemonium, from what I heard."

"After a while, families didn't like marrying off their children due to general contracts," Ron said. "So they mixed in the bride price."

"So they do buy the girls!" Hermione yelled.

"They exchanged heirlooms," Harry tried. "Books, jewellery. They mixed that into the contracts as an escape clause. Marry or pay gold. The family who wants out has to pay. Today the requirements are extremely limited, so contracts hardly ever apply."

"The girls aren't sold any more than the boys are," Seamus said. "Both parties know years beforehand and receive an update each year a hundred-and-forty-four days before their birthday. Enough time to pay the gold, shake hands and go their separate ways."

"Why a hundred-and-forty-four days?" Hermione asked, beating Dean to the punch.

"Gringotts got that duty after a goblin rebellion," Seamus replied. "The treaty was signed by twelve goblins and wizards each. After a goblin died overnight from a sudden dagger in the back, and a wizard from severe lack of head, they were even again. Twelve and twelve."

"Lovely," Hermione deadpanned. "But Harry wasn't informed beforehand, were you?"

He bit his lip. "I received no notification," he compromised. It was a truth, and likely the one that would cause the least trouble.

"There you have it," she called out triumphantly. "So they made a mistake, one Harry has no responsibility for. That should get him out of it!"

"The goblins send it exactly a hundred-and-forty-four days before the birthday," Ron pointed out. "Harry didn't inherit until summer. He would have been informed somewhere mid-March."

"Yeah," Seamus agreed. "You said you inherited the title of Head of House? Well, that should have bumped you up on the list. Or put you on it in the first place."

"And what list do you mean?" Hermione demanded impatiently.

"Candidates are ranked by relation to the family," Seamus explained. "Children of the House, Blacks in this case, are First Ranks. So is the likeliest candidate, even if they're just a child of a Black to another House – normally a Second Rank. First Ranks are named in the letters, so the other family knows who to talk to. Second Ranks are acceptable but unlikely; they get letters too, but only their number goes to the other family. Third Ranks aren't eligible, so they don't get letters."

Dean frowned. "How come Harry's stuck with it, then? He's a Potter, isn't he?"

"My grandmother was a Black. Another thing no one bothered to tell me." Harry sighed. "Half-blood grandson of a Black, I was a Third Rank. Safe, until the inheritance made me heir of House Black, on the same level as a son – a Second Rank. When I signed those papers, I was a Black for that moment, with enough authority to activate it. Greengrass was already an adult, so she was locked in as well. Just two teenagers stuck in a bad situation."

"She didn't buy out, then," Seamus observed, frowning. "Wait, did you say you were a Second Rank? Then someone else was meant to fulfil it?"

"There was another candidate for House Black, but he hadn't settled it either." Harry rolled his eyes at their looks. "Malfoy."

They blinked; then, Ron started laughing. "Oh, why hadn't you told me that? It's hilarious! You snatched up his bride-to-be? Ha, he must have been furious!"

Harry sighed. "Not sure about that. At least he isn't forced to marry some girl he hardly knows."

Hermione bit back a retort. "But you said something about contracts requiring purebloods..."

"From what I know of them, the Blacks would have put that in there, wouldn't they? Harry's not a pureblood."

Harry chuckled. "House Black demanded a pureblood from the other family. But House Black is toujours pure; even suggesting otherwise must have insulted Phineas Nigellus, the bloke who caused this trouble. A clause for his side would have been both insulting and redundant."

They let the silence linger. Ron seemed in conflict with himself, Seamus prepared his bed and Dean blinked a lot more than usual. Hermione paced. Finally, she stopped.

"So, what does that mean, then? There is no way out?" She looked to each of them.

"We wrote a personalized contract, and it's signed," Harry answered. "We kept it as simple as possible. Separate accounts, separate wills, separate lives. In summer we'll sign some paperwork, have a very small ceremony, and that's it."

"But... the wizarding world has divorces, right? You'll do that as soon as you are done, right after your wedding," Hermione tried, already guessing the answer.

"Divorces exist, yes, but they aren't that commonplace, not with love potions and stuff," Ron told her.

"And contracts do work a bit differently," Seamus added.

"Of course they do," Hermione sighed heavily, turning on the spot to return to pacing.

Harry smiled despite himself. "It's a magical contract, like the Triwizard Cup. That's why Greengrass couldn't simply borrow the gold to buy out – it's meant as a sacrifice in place of a child. You don't sacrifice someone else's child."

"But the families decide the clauses, so we included some escape routes. One of us losing their soul to the Dementors, for example. Same with life imprisonment."

"Not that I planned either for myself or her," Harry added. "They're just relatively uncomplicated clauses. Same with the heir clause," he finished, grinning widely.

Seamus laughed. "Brilliant! All your bases covered, I like that! Brilliant, Harry."

Hermione tapped her foot. Harry complied. "Should no heir be born within seven years, either side can walk away. All I had to do was not rule out the possibility of an heir at the time of signing."

Hermione thought it over. It was a testament to her that she hadn't started about the injustice of the clause as such. "So, unless you... err, produce an heir with Greengrass..."

"I will have an out after seven years. No heir, nothing forcing me to stay married. That's why we cut the Year of Yen, too."

To Hermione, Harry said, "The Year of Yen is a time for the newly-wed couple to... err... try to 'produce an heir'."

"Now you regret it, don't you?" Seamus teased. "But you should have insisted on a traditional wedding. The dancing figurines on the cake, the ram, the ewe..."

"They can stay at grass," Harry reasoned.

"Are you kidding?" Ron yelled. "We'll dig up the best traditions, and you'll have a wedding ceremony with everything!"

Neville nodded, smiling. "With the floral motif for the bridesmaids?"

"Floral..." Hermione asked, then continued, "Do I even want to know?"

Seamus smiled. "Traditionally, the bridesmaids' dresses are decorated with flowers. The Maid of Honour gets a bit more. The bride-groom's side is the same. Well, with the sheep there..."

"I get it," Hermione waved off. "I can't believe I'm saying this, but that does sound like fun."

Ron snorted. "And you were against it."

"I still think it's awful. But Harry seems to have it under control, relatively speaking, and there's an end in sight. But why seven years? Why not tomorrow?"

"The clauses cannot undermine the contract's purpose," Seamus explained. "An heir can't be produced by tomorrow, so that clause wouldn't take effect. Worse, any clauses linked to it might not matter either."

"Explanation?" Dean asked.

"I think I get it," Hermione told him. "If only children born in January and also Leos with black hair count, the first part is impossible. January and Leo cannot go together. If the clauses are linked, the hair restriction wouldn't work either. Any child would confirm it."

"Worse," Neville said, "without that clause, there might be no out at all, even if no child were born. Unless some unrelated clause allowed it, the marriage couldn't be dissolved. That's why the phrasing is important."

Seamus looked around. "As fun as this has been, I was getting to bed, so can we wrap it up? Harry, I'm guessing you don't want this public?"

He nodded. "That was kind of the idea, yes. There aren't that many people who know about it. The Weasleys do. Some bloke at the Ministry, too. The goblins. After this evening, everyone in this room."

"And her family," Neville pointed out.

"Yes, that's right. And the Malfoys," Harry continued. "I'm guessing her dorm mates as well, partly for the same reason I told you guys – to have a safe haven to talk about it away from nosy people."

"I feel loved," Dean yawned.

"Also, because you are my friends, yes," Harry added.

"When will the wedding be, then?" Hermione asked.

"Mid-August, after Bill's wedding. And we have already agreed: no ewe, no ram, no traditional wedding. This is a business deal."

Hermione nodded, but, ignoring the groans from Dean and Seamus, continued, "Why do the Malfoys know, incidentally?"

"They were informed that the contract had been activated. They were relieved of their obligation, after all," Harry told her.

"How did he take it?" Ron threw in. "Bet he didn't like it."

"Surprisingly well. Daphne said he seemed bored when they met on Christmas Day, after both got their owls. He seemed the same when I met him in Gringotts. We exchanged niceties and jabs and parted ways. It was strange to have him civil."

Ron asked, "Daphne?" at the same time Hermione said, "He did change over the school year, Harry, he's grown up."

He chuckled. "She said the same when I talked to her last time. And yes, Ron, Daphne, since there are numerous Greengrasses around, aren't there?"

"Well, she'll know him better than we do. If she thinks so too, then he really did change. It's not so unusual for people to grow up and become responsible."

"No, I think he's planning something." Harry narrowed his eyes. Malfoy, disinterested in losing gold and influence to his worst rival? He had always used power to mistreat others. Why stop? Perhaps losing his father had shocked him awake. All the pieces were there, but Harry couldn't put them together. He rather liked Moody for his paranoid streak; perhaps there was something to it. Something was off at Hogwarts.

"So," Hermione pulled Harry from his thoughts, counting on her fingers. "You are engaged to Greengrass, a Slytherin. The wedding is in August. You negotiated during the holidays. Even leaving out the Weasleys, a dozen people knew before tonight." Neville drew his curtains; Dean and Seamus covertly left. "And you waited for the right time to tell me, but hadn't found it yet? Because you didn't want to discuss it where we could have been overheard?"

Harry sent her an uneasy smile.
